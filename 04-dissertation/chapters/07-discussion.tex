\section{Answering the research questions}\label{sec:dis-rqs}
\textbf{RQ1 (edge competence): qualified, and the qualification is
topology-dependent.} At powered replication ($n{=}30$ disjoint seeds) the
picture splits. On Old Street, stock SLM control significantly beats the
fixed-time floor (up to $-11.8\%$, $p{<}0.001$) and one arm also beats
MaxPressure. On the measured-demand Euston corridor the same controllers
are 7--20\% \emph{worse} than the floor, significantly so on half the
arms --- a cell my exploratory three-seed sweep had reported as a win, and
the clearest evidence in this work for why powered replication on measured
demand was necessary. Fine-tuned control removes that corridor deficit
without becoming significantly better than the floor anywhere. The
defensible claim is therefore narrow: on-device SLM control is competent
within the deployable envelope on complex junction geometry, harmful on a
saturated arterial before distillation, and never demonstrably superior to
a naive fixed-time plan at $n{=}30$.

\textbf{RQ2 (what distillation buys): delay where the network can flow,
authorship everywhere, and it saturates at 0.6B.} Offline, distillation is
dramatic (55--69\% $\to$ 97--99\%) and scale-flat --- a 3.8B student adds
nothing over 0.6B, and no format specialist beats the generalist even on
its own format. Closed-loop the answer depends on whether the test network
is capable of clearing. On a demand-calibrated grid distillation is worth
$18.3\%$ delay against the identically-compiled stock model, on 30 of 30
seeds, and the two student scales sit within $\pm0.36\%$ of each other ---
so saturation is a bounded equivalence, not an absence of evidence. On
oversaturated or corridor maps the same manipulation yields little,
because the shield's envelope and the network's own saturation compress
every controller toward the same outcome. Independently of delay,
distillation buys \emph{ownership}: the SLM's
share of served actuations rises from 0.39--0.62 to 0.56--0.70, divergence
from MaxPressure among its own decisions falls four-fold, and the stock
harms (congested grid $+2.6\%$, $p{=}1.5{\times}10^{-3}$; measured-demand
corridor $+7$ to $+20\%$) are repaired to neutral. For a shielded, audited
controller I argue ownership is the criterion that generalises across
topologies, since it holds even where delay effects are compressed;
\S\ref{sec:dis-decoupling} explains when delay is and is not a sensitive
variable.

Two further results qualify the answer in opposite directions. The
saturation runs deeper than the accuracy tables alone show: on the
calibrated grid the two students sit within $\pm0.36\%$ of one another,
and across the wider sweep they reproduce each other's control trajectory
exactly on nine of ninety cells (\S\ref{sec:res-forensics}). A second,
larger student therefore buys little diversity, and an ensemble of the two
would be worth less than its cost. Against that, distillation is not free: on an
adjacent structured task the fine-tuned students are worse than their own
stock bases, one of them unable to emit the required output schema at all
(\S\ref{sec:res-lee}). The capability is not so much lost as
\emph{narrowed onto the deployed contract}, which is acceptable --- even
desirable --- for a component whose job is one decision type behind a
deterministic gate, and unacceptable if the same weights were expected to
serve the authorisation or legal-note roles as well.

\textbf{RQ3 (provable audit): yes, as designed.} Seven scenario
reconstructions prove end-to-end with tampering caught; the system emits
cited evidence packs, and the legally weak branches (authority fault) are
weak \emph{in the law}, faithfully represented rather than overstated.

\textbf{RQ4 (trust): partial, honestly --- and the partiality is
structured.} The mechanism's properties all hold; blatant misreporting is
detected and the liar's corroboration power stripped under every
controller tested, stock and fine-tuned alike, because the ledger scores
claims against local sensing rather than against model behaviour. Three
limits qualify that. Detection does not undo damage already done (a
caught blatant attack still costs up to eleven delay points on congested
grids); a calibrated stealth attacker is not detected at all, and its
payoff is positional --- retained trust it could later spend --- rather
than congestive; and the mechanism is inert where coordination traffic is
minimal, as on the corridor. The finding that most changes the design
picture is that the delay cost of a blatant attack is \emph{larger}
against fine-tuned controllers than stock ones: authorship cuts both
ways, because a model that genuinely acts on its inputs is moved further
by poisoned inputs. A trust ledger is therefore a necessary companion to
distillation rather than an optional layer, and it is not a sufficient
defence.

\section{Why accuracy decouples from delay}\label{sec:dis-decoupling}
Three mechanisms jointly explain the paradox, and all three are visible in
the data rather than conjectured. First, \emph{the shield compresses the
outcome distribution}: masking, substitution, and anti-starvation bound
the worst case for every arm, so the behavioural difference between a 59\%
and a 97\% proposer is far smaller served than raw. Second, \emph{the
headroom was small}: external calibration from a 13$\times$ larger tuned
model puts the entire LLM-vs-MaxPressure delay gap at 1--2\%
\cite{dglight2026}, and my own decision battery showed a near-chance
proposer winning closed-loop cells --- per-decision agreement with a
delay-optimal heuristic is simply not the trajectory-level objective.
Third, and decisively, \emph{the test network must be able to express the
difference}: on an oversaturated map every controller is compressed toward
the same terrible mean, and the same experiment on a network that clears
recovers an $18.3\%$ effect on every seed (\S\ref{sec:res-calib}). Two of
my three mechanisms are therefore properties of the measurement rather
than of the controller. The practical reading for deployment: fine-tune to
own the decisions and to remove harm tails, expect delay gains only where
the network is not already saturated, and never evaluate a controller on a
network that cannot clear.

\section{Threats to validity}\label{sec:dis-threats}
I declare four threats with their defences, and none is cosmetic.

\textbf{Label-only distillation (construct).} I distil teacher labels, not
reasoning traces; Orca-style trace distillation \cite{mukherjee2023orca}
might transfer more competence. The myopic format makes the cost
measurable: its $\sim$88\% ceiling is the privileged-teacher gap,
disclosed as a design property.

\textbf{Retention (internal).} Aggressive SFT can erode general
capability with scale-dependent forgetting \cite{forgetting2024}. I
measured it: a seeded 200-item MMLU sample served through the same
endpoints shows the 3.8B student dropping from 57.5\% (stock) to 53.0\%
(fine-tuned) --- a decline consistent with mild forgetting but not
significant at this sample size ($z{=}0.91$, $p{=}.37$) --- while
retaining its answer-format discipline (94\% strict JSON). At 0.6B the
probe is uninformative by floor effect: the stock model already scores at
chance (26.0\%) on this format, so there was no measurable general
capability to lose. The honest conclusion is that no significant
retention damage was detected on general knowledge, with the caveat that
a 200-item probe bounds only large effects. That conclusion should not be
read as reassurance, because a nearer-neighbour probe tells a harsher
story: on the authorisation task (\S\ref{sec:res-lee}) both fine-tuned
students fall well below their own stock bases, one failing to produce
the required output schema at all. The damage is not diffuse forgetting
of world knowledge but narrowing onto the trained contract --- which
general-knowledge benchmarks are poorly placed to detect, and which
matters more for a deployment that might later ask the same weights to
do a second job.

\textbf{Quantisation scope (construct).} ``Lossless'' int4 is claimed
strictly as bit-identical holdout accuracy under weight-only RTN --- not
as a general equivalence claim, which the quantisation literature would
not support \cite{quantlora2024,frantar2022gptq}.

\textbf{Simulation and demand realism (external).} Everything is SUMO;
real-map demand magnitudes are measured (DfT hourly profile on Euston) but
their vehicle-level realisations are synthetic draws, and comparators
exclude trained deep-RL systems. The MaxPressure comparison is
accordingly reported without a general claim: the three-seed reversal did
not replicate at thirty paired seeds (\S\ref{sec:res-maxpressure}), six
grid studies showing MaxPressure's advantage are cited, and my own grids
reproduce their direction. Legal
scope: the audit scenarios use synthetic actors on real topology, so no
real data subject exists; a real deployment would face the adjudication
and disclosure exposures analysed in the project's legal grounding, which
curation itself is an adjudicative act (\emph{Bates v Post Office (No 6)})
and qualified privilege is defeasible.

\section{Limitations and future work}\label{sec:dis-future}
Four directions follow directly from the findings. \textbf{Formal shield
synthesis}: replace the heuristic composite with a synthesized shield from
a temporal-logic safety specification \cite{alshiekh2018shield} --- the
per-junction product game is small enough for exact synthesis, and the
authorship metrics defined here would then carry minimal-interference
guarantees. \textbf{Closed-loop objectives}: GRPO-style tuning against
trajectory reward \cite{dglight2026} attacks the decoupling directly;
label distillation is the wrong objective if delay is the goal.
\textbf{Stealth-robust trust}: detection calibrated to distributional
implausibility rather than single-report bounds. \textbf{Hardware-in-the-loop}:
the serving-reliability findings (service self-restarts, artifact
corruption) are exactly the failure class a cabinet deployment must
survive, and they argue for a watchdog-supervised serving architecture
before any field pilot.

\subsection{Deployment vectors this architecture is suited to}
The evaluation above measures a permanent-junction scenario, which is the
hardest case for this controller: a permanent junction already has a
fixed-time plan engineered offline from historical counts, so matching it
buys little. Three deployment settings invert that arithmetic, and I set
them out with the evidence that supports each and the work each would
still require.

\textbf{Temporary and roadworks signals.} Portable signals at a lane
closure have no engineered plan; they run a guessed cycle or simple
vehicle-actuation. Against that baseline, a controller that needs
\emph{no site configuration} is worth more than one that matches a tuned
plan. Three properties measured here transfer: the controller derives its
entire phase model from the live network at start-up
(\S\ref{sec:des-overview}), so it is not configured per site; the
leave-one-topology-out student matched the generalist on the topology
withheld from its training (\S\ref{sec:res-offline}), which is the closest
evidence I have for a layout the model has never seen; and the
anti-starvation floor guarantees alternation deterministically, which is
the core safety requirement of single-lane shuttle working. Three gaps are
equally clear: I tested no lane-closure or shuttle-working layout at all;
the phase model is read once at start-up, so a mid-deployment
reconfiguration would require re-initialisation rather than being handled
live; and shuttle working imposes a clearance-time constraint, that a
vehicle must exit the single-lane section before the opposing green, which
belongs in the deterministic shield and is not implemented. The
architecture is suited to this case; the evidence is suggestive rather
than demonstrative, and I claim no more.

\textbf{Emergency-vehicle priority across a corridor.} Conventional
installations without emergency-vehicle detection give an approaching
emergency vehicle no priority, and \S\ref{sec:res-ev} measures that
omission as worth 31.1\,s per event on this corridor. The coordination
substrate for doing better already exists in the implementation: junctions
exchange Ed25519-signed state reports, and the transport has an MQTT
adapter that puts a real broker between publisher and subscriber while
reusing the same signing, adjacency, registry and replay checks as the
in-process bus. What is missing before this is a deployment claim rather
than a capability claim: the signed bus has been exercised in simulation
and over a local broker, not over a field radio or cellular backhaul under
loss and latency; the emergency scenario is scripted, so the per-event
benefit is not an expected value under naturalistic call arrival rates;
and corridor-scale progressive preemption, clearing a path several
junctions ahead of the vehicle, is designed for but unmeasured.

\textbf{Deployment economics.} Nothing in this dissertation costs the
system. A serious feasibility case would need a bill of materials for an
edge node at each junction, a comparison against the installed cost of
conventional adaptive control, the backhaul and broker infrastructure the
signed bus requires, and the power envelope, which my 6\,GB desktop GPU
does not represent. That analysis needs current market data rather than
inference from this study, so I flag it as required future work rather
than estimate it here. It is the analysis that would decide whether the
temporary-signal case above is commercially as well as technically
attractive.
