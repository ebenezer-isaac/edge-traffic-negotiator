\subsection{Seeds and dataset splits}
\textbf{Training-state seeds:} $\{3, 7, 11, 29, 42, 57, 91, 123\}$. Junction states for
distillation were sampled only from these.
\textbf{Closed-loop evaluation seeds:}
$\{1..30\}\setminus\{3,7,11,29\}\cup\{31,32,33,34\}$ gives thirty seeds
disjoint from training (\S\ref{sec:met-disjoint}).
\textbf{Offline holdout:} split by deterministic content hash
(\texttt{HOLDOUT\_MOD}=12) and frozen before the first training run, which
leaves 5{,}580 held-out examples against 59{,}533 training examples, from
65{,}113 accepted renderings (6 delay-aware states rejected by the sanity
verifier for serving an empty phase without a justifying note, removing
their sota and myopic renderings). Per format: sota 16{,}982, myopic
16{,}982, prediction 18{,}596, coordination 12{,}553.

\subsection{Prompt contract}
All four formats share one output contract: exactly
\texttt{\{"phase": N\}}. The \emph{same rendering code serves training,
offline evaluation, and closed-loop control}, so no format skew can
flatter the offline numbers. The delay-aware (sota) system prompt is
reproduced in part:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily\raggedright
You control ONE traffic-signal junction (this junction only; no neighbour
information). For each green phase you are given: the number of waiting
(queued) vehicles, the TOTAL accumulated waiting time of those vehicles in
seconds, and whether it is the phase currently green. Choose the phase to
serve next to MINIMISE total vehicle waiting time and unnecessary stops -{}-
not simply the longest queue.
\end{quote}

The myopic format withholds accumulated waiting, prediction adds
short-horizon arrival counts, and coordination adds neighbour release
notes. Parsing tiers are as given in \S\ref{sec:des-agent}.

\subsection{The compile-and-serve chain}
Reproducing a served custom model requires the following steps in order,
each established by failure during this project.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Train QLoRA adapters ($r{=}16$, NF4 double quantisation, fp16
compute, completion-only loss, family-correct chat template).
\item Merge adapters to a bf16 checkpoint.
\item Compile with the onnxruntime-genai model builder
(\texttt{-p int4 -e cpu}) using the pinned transformers pairing, because
newer environments fail on attention-module or loss-utility imports.
\item Patch \texttt{tokenizer\_config.json}: \texttt{tokenizer\_class}
must be a value the runtime accepts (\texttt{GPT2Tokenizer}), because
transformers-5 era configs emit a backend name that fails to load.
\item For Phi checkpoints, strip remote-code \texttt{auto\_map} entries.
\item \textbf{Synthesise} \texttt{inference\_model.json} (model name plus
prompt template). The builder emits none, and without it the model
registers but returns HTTP 400 on every request.
\item Copy into the Foundry Local cache directory and, for GPU serving,
set \texttt{provider\_options} to WebGPU in \texttt{genai\_config.json}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Harness invariants}
Three properties of the experiment harness underwrite the results.
\emph{Fail-loud:} API errors are counted and abort a run rather than being
scored as answers. An unservable model records a skip, never a score.
\emph{Resumable:} cells are keyed by (model, config, topology, seed) and
skipped if already measured, so a crash loses at most one cell. One
zero-byte cell caused by a mid-write power loss was detected by JSON
validation and re-run.
\emph{Recoverable:} after two observed mid-run service self-restarts, the
offline evaluator rediscovers the endpoint, reloads the model, and retries
failed rows with statistics intact.

\section{UK-law fault-grounding knowledge base}\label{app:uklaw}

The reconstruction layer matches verified collision facts against a
curated knowledge base of UK traffic law. The base is deliberately small,
explicit, and citable: each rule carries a statute or case reference, a
binding strength (\texttt{MUST} for statutory prohibitions,
\texttt{common\_law} for judicial doctrine), a fault weight, and the
verified fact that triggers it. Table~\ref{tab:uklaw} lists the rules
exercised by the seven evaluated scenarios.

\begin{table*}[t]

\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.28\linewidth}p{0.26\linewidth}p{0.36\linewidth}}
\toprule
Rule & Authority & Effect \\
\midrule
Driver crossed red & RTA 1988 s36 & Criminal offence; driver fault high \\
Red prohibition & TSRGD 2016 Sch14 & Must not proceed beyond stop line \\
Amber & TSRGD 2016 5(9) & Driver fault high where a safe stop was possible \\
EV exemption & TSRGD / conditional & Conditional on an emergency crossing
class \emph{plus} either corroboration or a valid emergency-class key;
endangerment test still applies \\
Maintenance vehicle & no exemption & Works vehicle treated as an ordinary driver \\
Dark signal & Highway Code r.176 & Duty to obey falls away; ordinary care governs \\
Authority misfeasance & \emph{Bird v Pearce} & Positively-wrong indication:
authority fault non-zero but low \\
Authority nonfeasance & \emph{Gorringe v Calderdale} & Mere omission: authority
fault defaults to zero \\
EV apportionment anchor & \emph{Griffin v Mersey} & Default 60/40 against the

Green due regard & TSRGD 2016 Sch14 Pt1 5(14) & Green-light party must proceed

One property of the encoding matters for the accountability claim: the
emergency exemption is gated first on the mechanically verified crossing
class, which a forged key cannot manufacture, and only then on
corroboration or a valid emergency-class key, so a spoofed claim leaves
the driver squarely within the red-light prohibition
(\S\ref{sec:res-audit-worked}). Legal causation is never assessed but
marked \texttt{NOT\_ASSESSED} for a human adjudicator.
