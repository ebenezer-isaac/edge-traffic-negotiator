\subsection{Classical signal control and its guarantees}\label{sec:bg-classical}

Deployed urban signal control rests on two families. Fixed-time control serves a cycle engineered offline from historical counts. It optimises nothing online, but it is deterministic, starvation-free and indifferent to sensing noise. Its adaptive rival MaxPressure \cite{varaiya2013maxpressure}, the canonical strong baseline, serves at each intersection the phase of greatest ``pressure'' (upstream minus downstream queues, weighted by turn ratios), a decentralised rule Varaiya proved throughput-optimal for every stabilisable demand. That guarantee rests on four assumptions, and read against the backpressure literature (Appendix~\ref{app:backpressure}) each fails structurally in central London. The proof assumes unbounded queue storage; short links violate it, and the finite-capacity repair \cite{gregoire2014backpressure} is not part of standard MaxPressure. The only stability result for a deployable controller holds under heavy load \cite{gregoire2014routing}, which measured diurnal demand sits below for most of the day. Every favourable empirical figure comes from a grid. And service in the theorems is binary and phase-determined, which give-way, gap-acceptance and gyratory junctions break. I do not measure the first two conditions on my networks.

The field's own tables corroborate this. AttendLight \cite{oroojlooy2020attendlight} reports MaxPressure \emph{losing} to naive fixed-time by 4.6--18.6\% on some T-junctions and two to four times on its two four-way layouts with unequal lane counts (INT10, INT11), a geometry-specific collapse. Against this, at least six independent studies \cite{wei2019colight,lai2023llmlight,wang2025trafficr1,dglight2026,collmlight2025,curalight2026} show it beating fixed-time by 7--58\% on grid-like CityFlow and SUMO networks, including nominally ``real'' Manhattan, Jinan and Hangzhou topologies, all on synthetic or replayed-uniform demand. My measurements (\S\ref{sec:res-maxpressure}) fall between: the sign changes with the geometry, while my synthetic grids behave conventionally (n=3). That pre-empts the objection that my baseline is broken, and it stops short of the stronger reversal an earlier three-seed sweep had suggested. From a targeted rather than systematic review, I find no prior work testing MaxPressure against fixed-time on measured UK geometry under measured demand.
