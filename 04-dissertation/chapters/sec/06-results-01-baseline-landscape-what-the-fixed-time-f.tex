\subsection{Baseline landscape: what the fixed-time floor revealed}\label{sec:res-maxpressure}
In the exploratory sweep (three seeds per cell, daily-resolution demand) the
theoretically throughput-optimal MaxPressure \cite{varaiya2013maxpressure}
appeared \emph{worse than naive fixed-time on every real London topology}
(Euston $+15\%$, Old Street $+5\%$, Bloomsbury $+3\%$) yet dominated the
synthetic grids ($-27\%$ on grid4$\times$4, $-19\%$ on grid3$\times$3).
Replication kills it: Table~\ref{tab:mpfloor} recomputes it paired per seed
over the thirty training-disjoint evaluation seeds.

At $n{=}30$ the picture is a \finding{sign flip that tracks geometry}
(Figure~\ref{fig:signflip}), not a reversal: on Old Street, the study's most complex geometry at five arms
and seventeen movements, MaxPressure is \emph{significantly better} than
fixed-time, while on the oversaturated Bloomsbury grid the two are
indistinguishable; calibrated to clear, the same network puts MaxPressure
$8.8\%$ ahead, so every ``tied on Bloomsbury'' statement is scoped to the
oversaturated regime. Calibration does not settle the corridor, where the
same rule leaves the comparison inconclusive ($p{=}.095$, a CI straddling
zero, absolute means favouring MaxPressure by $2.4\%$),\footnote{The
$+14.2\%$ is the mean of per-seed ratios, the convention used throughout
this section, and it is inflated by a single $+140.3\%$ seed. On absolute
paired delays the same comparison gives $p{=}.82$. The row is reported
because the convention is applied uniformly, not because it supports a
direction.} so saturation is unconfirmed as a \emph{sufficient} account of
MaxPressure's weakness: the networks differ in several respects at once and
nothing here varies them independently. Before calibration MaxPressure is
\emph{significantly worse} than fixed-time on the saturated corridor too,
with a caveat: its per-seed spread is heavily skewed, and on absolute
rather than relative delays the paired test would not reach significance
($p{=}.083$), making Euston the least robust of the three real networks to
how the question is posed.

A third classical baseline, longest-queue-first, which serves the largest
upstream queue with no downstream term, is statistically indistinguishable
from MaxPressure on all five topologies ($-0.9\%$ to $+13.4\%$, every
$p\ge.30$) and reproduces its pattern against the naive floor
(Appendix~\ref{app:lqf}), so \finding{the downstream term buys nothing
measurable}; the intervals are wide, so this is a non-rejection rather than
a demonstrated equivalence, and saturation, link storage and the assumed
turning split all remain candidates.

MaxPressure's advantage is uniform and substantial only on the regular
synthetic grids the literature favours, which replication did not reach:
those figures remain three-seed. Backpressure theory suggests why the
arterial is its weakest case, a saturated short-link corridor violating
both the unbounded-storage and heavy-load assumptions of
\S\ref{sec:bg-classical} \cite{gregoire2014backpressure,gregoire2014routing};
that is a hypothesis consistent with the Euston direction, not a
demonstrated explanation, since nothing here manipulates storage capacity.
Every controller claim is therefore stated against the fixed-time floor,
with MaxPressure alongside.
