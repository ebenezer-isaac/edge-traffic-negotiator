\subsection{Base-model factorial}\label{sec:res-factorial}
The base-model sweep (eight servable catalogue models $\times$ four prompt
formats $\times$ six topologies) established three facts before any
fine-tuning. It is a sweep, not a full factorial. Three models were never
swept on the later measured-demand corridor, phi-4-mini-reasoning is
latency-excluded (\S\ref{sec:met-baselines}) after a single-seed corridor
probe, and the ANOVA is fitted on the balanced subset; the 6\,GB card caps
the ladder at $\sim$4B (\S\ref{sec:imp-foundry}).

The sweep ran three seeds per cell with MaxPressure as its only
comparator. In it the best shielded stock arm beat MaxPressure on every
topology, from $-2.4\%$ on grid4$\times$4 to $-31.0\%$ on the daily-demand
corridor (Bloomsbury $-6.1\%$, Old Street $-15.4\%$). Those are per-map
bests. Averaged over all stock arms the sweep is far worse than MaxPressure
on the synthetic grids ($+20\%$ and $+36\%$) and mixed on the real maps,
from $-15.8\%$ on the daily-demand corridor to $+0.1\%$ on Euston
peak-hour, and the per-map win does \emph{not} survive replication on the
measured-demand corridor. It motivated the powered design rather than
counting as evidence. Results must also be conditioned on topology,
because pooling across maps is a Simpson's-paradox trap. And per-decision
reasoning quality does not predict closed-loop value across base models.
On 80 identical junction states cloud frontier models agree with the
delay-optimal heuristic at 92--96\% against 51--72\% for local SLMs, yet a
near-chance local model still won closed-loop cells; within one base it
can (\S\ref{sec:res-calib}). The controller's value is plausibly systemic,
SLM, shield and gating together, though no arm removes the shield or the
gate to test that, and this battery is open-loop by construction.

An explanatory two-way ANOVA (2 models $\times$ 4 topologies $\times$ 3
seeds, config fixed) confirms the structure, topology dominating (partial
$\eta^2{=}0.97$), the model mattering (partial $\eta^2{=}0.83$) and the two
interacting strongly ($F{=}32.2$, $p{<}0.001$, partial $\eta^2{=}0.86$),
though at three seeds per cell this is exploratory, not the powered design.

The powered stock-model replication ($n{=}30$ per cell, seeds 1--30, four
catalogue models $\times$ two formats, both baselines) sharpens this per
topology; stock models have no training set, so training-disjoint seeds are
not required. On \textbf{Old Street} stock SLM control achieves the
campaign's strongest powered wins against the fixed-time floor:
Qwen2.5-0.5B sota $-11.8\%$ ($p{<}0.001$), Qwen3-0.6B sota $-9.6\%$
($p{<}0.001$), Phi-4-mini myopic $-8.6\%$ ($p{<}0.001$), one arm also
significantly beating MaxPressure (Qwen2.5-0.5B sota, $-4.9\%$,
$p{=}.028$).

On \textbf{Euston peak-hour}, with demand set to the DfT-measured
peak-hour magnitude, directional split and vehicle mix for the A501 count
points (turning proportions assumed, single weekday survey), the result
reverses. Every stock arm is \risk{worse than the fixed-time floor by
$7.4\%$ to $19.7\%$}, significantly on three of eight arms (two-sided $p$
.017--.026, a fourth at $p{=}.053$), yet statistically indistinguishable
from MaxPressure. That is not a null but a measured harm. The measured
magnitude loads the corridor \emph{less} heavily than the daily average it
replaces (780 vehicles against 1{,}071, 45.3\% completing against 33.1\%).
Whether the lighter load is what makes the harm visible cannot be said
here, because reducing Euston's saturation further does not behave as a
saturation-compression account predicts (\S\ref{sec:res-calib}).
\risk{Distillation does not repair this}: the fine-tuned Qwen and Phi arms
are $+15.5\%$ and $+16.2\%$ against the same comparator
(Table~\ref{tab:closedloop-full}), inside the stock band. An earlier draft
claimed the opposite, an artefact of scoring fine-tuned arms against
MaxPressure and stock arms against fixed-time. Whether the deficit is
topological or a residue of congestion that calibration could not remove
is left open in \S\ref{sec:res-calib}.

The replication also yielded an unplanned methodological finding. On the
26 seeds shared between runs, the \emph{catalogue} Qwen3-0.6B artifact and
my identically-prompted, identically-shielded compile of the same weights
differ by nine points of mean relative delay ($+4.1\%$ vs $-5.2\%$ against
MaxPressure) on this high-variance topology. Quantisation and packaging
provenance alone shift the headline sign (Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}).
Hence the identical-pipeline control of \S\ref{sec:met-baselines}: ``the
same model'' is not the same controller across serving artifacts.
