\subsection{Offline distillation: saturation at 0.6B}\label{sec:res-offline}
Table~\ref{tab:offline-full} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) gives the complete
offline campaign on the frozen 5{,}580-state holdout as served (WebGPU,
int4): both student scales, stock versus fine-tuned through the identical
int4 pipeline, plus LOTO and format-specialist controls;
Figure~\ref{fig:offline} plots the four main rows.

\input{chapters/sec/fig-offline}

Two Qwen checkpoints appear in this report: the tabled row is the
\emph{generalist} student, trained and evaluated on all four formats,
while every closed-loop result uses the \emph{sota-only} student, trained
on the delay-aware format alone and served in it, which scores 97.4\%
against the generalist's 97.7\% on the only format either is asked to act
in. The offline table therefore characterises the distillation rather than
the provenance of the closed-loop arms. Five findings follow.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \finding{Scale saturation.} The 0.6B and 3.8B generalists are
statistically indistinguishable on every format: the largest gap, 0.8
points on myopic, sits against a standard error of about 1.2 points on
$n{=}1{,}510$, so no two-proportion test approaches significance. That
test is conservative, since both students are scored on the same frozen
items, but only aggregate accuracies per format were retained, making the
exact paired test (McNemar) uncomputable after the fact. Both sit at the
label-noise ceiling on three of four formats (97 to 99\% against 97.3 to
99.3\% agreement between the two frontier models that produced the labels,
on 600 cross-checked items), myopic excepted at 88\%. Six times the
parameters buy no measurable per-decision gain.
\item \finding{Where the gain lands depends on the base.} The 0.6B is
lifted by $+29.7$ to $+42.6$ points on every format; Phi-4-mini, already
100\% format-compliant, gains most on the delay-aware sota format
($+38.1$) and negligibly on myopic ($+1.2$), so for a capable base
fine-tuning raises decision quality from an already high floor (87.5,
59.0, 86.0, 87.0) rather than instilling format discipline.
\item \finding{Generalists only.} No format specialist beats the generalist
on its own format by more than $0.7$ points while off-format costs run from
$0.8$ to $9.2$ points, and the myopic specialist loses \emph{even
on-format} (87.0 vs 88.7), consistent with mixed-format training
regularising against noisy privileged-teacher labels; the sweep ran at
3.8B only, and the sota-only Qwen student is not part of it.
\item \finding{No detectable topology memorisation.} The LOTO student,
trained with all Old Street states removed, matches the generalist on every
format; Old Street is 4.2\% of the sota rows and 0.4\% of coordination, so
this bounds a held-out-topology deficit near 7 points rather than measuring
one, and per-topology accuracy was not retained.
\item \finding{Quantised serving costs no measurable accuracy here.} The
CPU and WebGPU int4 artifacts score identically in aggregate (97.42\% both
on the sota holdout, $n{=}1{,}510$). Per-item outputs were not retained, so
this is equality of accuracy, not demonstrated output identity, and the
``lossless'' claim is scoped narrowly in \S\ref{sec:dis-threats}.
\end{enumerate}
The myopic ceiling ($\sim$88\%) is most likely intrinsic to the format
rather than a training failure: the teacher was privileged with
waiting-time state the myopic prompt hides, an Orca-style label-only
distillation cost \cite{mukherjee2023orca}, and every myopic arm bar the
stock 0.6B lands within 1.7 points of that ceiling, consistent with a
label-noise floor; the non-privileged-label control that would establish
it was not run.
