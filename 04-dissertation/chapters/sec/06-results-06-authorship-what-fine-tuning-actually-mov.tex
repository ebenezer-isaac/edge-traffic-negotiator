\subsection{Authorship: what fine-tuning actually moves}\label{sec:res-authorship}
The structural columns of Table~\ref{tab:closedloop-full} carry the
report's central finding. It emerges only if the three denominators of
\S\ref{sec:des-shield} are kept apart (full composition per cell:
Table~\ref{tab:composition}, Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

Any claim that an SLM arm ``matches'' MaxPressure would be circular were
the shield quietly substituting the classical phase. \finding{It is not.}
Across fourteen arm-by-topology cells and 420 seed-runs the substitution
path fired in exactly one cell, the fine-tuned arm on the Bloomsbury grid,
at 0.54\% of decisions on average (110 in total, 8.99\% on its worst
single seed). Every other cell is 0.00\% on every seed
(\texttt{shield\_fallbacks\_none} is zero in every SLM cell).

The anti-starvation floor is the large non-SLM component, at 23 to 61\% of
ticks, and it authors 24 to 43\% of the \emph{MaxPressure} arm on the same
maps. The rule is identical in every arm but its share is not. Paired per
seed, the stock arm carries 4 to 18 points more floor than MaxPressure on
every map ($p\le1.2{\times}10^{-9}$), while the fine-tuned arm sits at the
MaxPressure rate. So the floor intervenes most in the arm that performs
worst, and what fine-tuning removes is the stock proposer's excess over
the no-SLM rate.

\finding{Fine-tuning raises the SLM's true authorship of actuations}, most
where stock was weakest: Old Street 0.39 to 0.56, Bloomsbury 0.62 to 0.70
(0.76 once calibrated), Euston 0.62 to 0.64 (Figure~\ref{fig:authorship}).
The floor column is the arithmetic complement (Old Street 0.61 $\to$
0.44), so it adds no independent evidence. Proposal acceptance, which
does, rises with it (0.54 $\to$ 0.90). The natural reading is that the
distilled model serves neglected approaches before the fairness timer
must. That cannot be separated from the arm simply tripping the timer less
often, because the logs record only who served each tick, not which
approach. The honest headline is the authored share, not the acceptance
rate: even the best fine-tuned arm leaves \emph{23 to 44\% of actuations
authored by a hard-coded timer}.

Cutting against the simple reading, \risk{the fine-tuned arms land level
with MaxPressure while they choose what MaxPressure would have chosen}.
Divergence among SLM-authored decisions falls from 0.46--0.60 to
0.11--0.17, and on the calibrated networks that carry the headline it
falls to 0.04 and 0.07, the two lowest in the study, with authorship at
0.59 against 0.76--0.77. That is genuine authorship of a near-classical
policy. But the deflationary reading is strongest exactly where the delay
result is largest: distillation may have taught the student to imitate the
classical reference, an explanation neither established nor excluded. Two
observations bound it. The teacher was a frontier model reasoning over
accumulated waiting, not MaxPressure, and the students agree with its
held-out labels at 97--99\% (\S\ref{sec:res-offline}), so any convergence
is at most indirect. And closed-loop delay cannot discriminate imitation
from rediscovery: against the fixed-time comparator MaxPressure is
$+22.3\%$ on Euston peak-hour against the fine-tuned arm's $+15.5\%$, and
on the calibrated grid the two coincide within $0.03\%$. Only the
remaining one-in-ten divergences, where the arms differ from both
baselines, could discriminate them. I have not characterised those, so the
question needs a decision-level counterfactual study I did not run. What
survives is the audit-relevant claim at its true magnitude: over the stock
Old Street deployment an accident audit could attribute 39\% of actuations
to the learned component, over the fine-tuned one 56\%, the balance
falling to a deterministic timer. That is a smaller claim than \emph{the
model is the controller}.
