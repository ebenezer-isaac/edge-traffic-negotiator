\subsection{Audit and crash reconstruction}\label{sec:res-audit}
The audit layer was evaluated on seven pre-defined collision scenarios
spanning the legally distinct cases (red-light running, EV exemption,
maintenance vehicle, amber gambling, conflicting green, dark signal,
spoofed EV). All seven prove end-to-end: hash chain verified, signatures
verified, and an imposter re-signing attack caught in every scenario. The
governing UK rule is \emph{inferred deterministically from the verified
facts}: spoofed-EV correctly denies the exemption and anchors fault on the
driver, and conflicting green alone attributes (weak, misfeasance-only)
authority fault. The output is a cited evidence pack for a human
adjudicator, never a verdict \cite{dahl2024legal}. Above the per-run
chain, the Certificate-Transparency-style completeness layer of
\S\ref{sec:bg-audit} passes its property battery over a twelve-entry
batch, including detection of a witness equivocation, with two limits: the
cross-auditor's own honesty is an \risk{unverified assumption}, and the
batch is \risk{not externally anchored} (Appendix~\ref{app:ct}).

\subsubsection{A worked evidence pack}\label{sec:res-audit-worked}
Figure~\ref{fig:evpack} traces the hardest of the seven end to end, a
collision at a junction that emitted a conflicting green. Each cited rule
carries the fact that triggered it, so an investigator can audit the
inference rather than read a conclusion, and the doctrine cited runs
against the deployer.
