\section{Audit and crash reconstruction}\label{sec:res-audit}
The audit layer was evaluated on seven pre-defined collision scenarios
spanning the legally distinct cases (red-light running, EV exemption,
maintenance vehicle, amber gambling, conflicting green, dark signal,
spoofed EV). All seven prove end-to-end: hash chain verified, signatures
verified, and an imposter re-signing attack caught in every scenario. The
governing UK rule is \emph{inferred deterministically from the verified
facts}: spoofed-EV correctly denies the exemption and anchors fault on
the driver, and conflicting green alone attributes (weak,
misfeasance-only) authority fault. The output is a cited evidence pack
for a human adjudicator, never a verdict \cite{dahl2024legal}.

Above the per-run chain sits the Certificate-Transparency-style
completeness layer of \S\ref{sec:bg-audit}. Over a twelve-entry batch, a salted Merkle root hides the entry
contents while remaining provable, the salt is erasable off-chain, and an inclusion
proof verifies while the same proof under a \emph{forged} salt is rejected. Under a
two-witness cross-audit the witnesses agree and the batch is consistent. When one
witness is made to serve a different root, \finding{the equivocation is detected and
consistency fails}, and a witness refuses to overwrite its own history. The
cross-auditor's own honesty is an \risk{unverified assumption}, on a par
with the identity root. One further limit: the mechanism passes but the
batch is \risk{not externally anchored}. I withheld the public-log write
rather than pollute a production transparency log.

\subsection{A worked evidence pack}\label{sec:res-audit-worked}
Figure~\ref{fig:evpack} traces the hardest of the seven end to end: a
collision at a junction that emitted a conflicting green.

Each cited rule carries the fact that triggered it, so an investigator can
audit the inference rather than read a conclusion, and the doctrine cited
runs against the deployer.
