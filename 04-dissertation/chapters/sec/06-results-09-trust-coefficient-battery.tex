\subsection{Trust-coefficient battery}\label{sec:res-trust}
The mechanism passes its 9/9 property battery (\S\ref{sec:des-trust}). In
closed-loop traffic I ran it against fine-tuned 3.8B, stock 0.6B and
fine-tuned 0.6B controllers, at three seeds per cell and with gating off,
so the figures are descriptive and not comparable cell-for-cell with
Table~\ref{tab:closedloop-full}. Appendix~\ref{app:trust} gives the
detail. Three findings follow. \finding{Detection is
controller-independent by design and in measurement}: every controller
catches and collapses a blatant liar where lies are injectable, while a
calibrated stealth attacker evades essentially everywhere, an open
limitation. \finding{The delay cost of blatant lying survives detection},
adding $+2.7$ to $+11.5$ points on the synthetic grids, because detection
bounds future influence without undoing congestion. And
\finding{stealth's harm is positional, not congestive}. The threat model
for a fine-tuned deployment is therefore asymmetric, and that is the
deployment that matters, since a controller acting on its coordination
inputs is what poisoned inputs can move (\S\ref{sec:res-authorship}).
Distributional detection is the right next defence
(\S\ref{sec:dis-future}).
