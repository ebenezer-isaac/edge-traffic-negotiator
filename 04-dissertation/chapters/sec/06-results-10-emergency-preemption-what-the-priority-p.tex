\section{Emergency preemption: what the priority path is worth}\label{sec:res-ev}
The authorisation machinery serves a concrete function, measured over
thirty seeds on the synthetic arterial corridor with a scripted
ambulance and spoofing attack, in three modes: \emph{nopreempt}
(no signal priority,
as at an installation without emergency-vehicle detection),
\emph{naive} (preempts on any claim), and \emph{defended} (preempts only
on a claim with a valid authorising key and corroboration,
\S\ref{sec:des-trust}).

All four intervals exclude zero. \finding{Signal priority is worth about
half a minute to an emergency vehicle} against a no-priority controller,
\finding{security has a measurable price}, and \finding{that price buys
quantified protection}. Whether that
exchange is worth making is a policy judgement. Scope: one
scripted ambulance and one attack window per run, so these are
per-event magnitudes, not expected values under a naturalistic call rate,
and the baseline characterises no specific deployed system.
