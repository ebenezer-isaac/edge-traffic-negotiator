\begin{abstract}
Large-language-model traffic signal controllers are evaluated in the cloud;
junctions run at the edge, and after an accident someone must prove which
controller did what. We present, to our knowledge, the first traffic signal
controller fine-tuned and served entirely on-device: a QLoRA-distilled small
language model (SLM) compiled to int4 ONNX and served by Microsoft Foundry
Local on a 6\,GB consumer GPU, controlling measured London topologies in SUMO
behind a MaxPressure safety shield with a tamper-evident, Ed25519 hash-chained
decision audit. Distilling a frontier teacher lifts held-out decision accuracy
from 55--69\% to 97--99\% and \emph{saturates by 0.6B parameters}: a 3.8B
student is statistically indistinguishable from a 0.6B one. Closed-loop, on
30 training-disjoint seeds per topology, this accuracy does \emph{not}
translate into significant delay improvement over a fixed-time baseline ---
but fine-tuning cuts the shield override rate from 0.46--0.60 to 0.11--0.18,
raising the SLM's authored share of served decisions to as much as 0.90, and
repairs the one significant harm the stock model causes (grid congestion,
$+2.6\%$ delay, $p{=}1.5{\times}10^{-3}$, to neutral). We argue authorship is
the property that matters: an accident audit of a controller whose decisions
are mostly shield substitutions attributes nothing to the learned component.
Distillation makes the audit \emph{about the model}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
LLM-based traffic signal control has moved quickly from prompting to
fine-tuning \cite{lai2023llmlight,wang2025trafficr1,curalight2026}, but the
deployment story has not moved with it: every published controller is
evaluated against a cloud endpoint, while the signal cabinet at a junction is
an edge device with tight memory, no service-level guarantee to the cloud, and
--- uniquely among LLM application domains --- a statutory duty to explain
itself after a collision. A junction controller that cannot be audited is not
deployable regardless of its delay numbers.

This paper asks what fine-tuning actually contributes to an SLM traffic
controller that runs \emph{entirely} on-device under a classical safety
shield. We fine-tune with QLoRA \cite{dettmers2023qlora} on decisions
distilled from a frontier teacher, compile to int4 ONNX, and serve with
Microsoft Foundry Local on an RTX~2060 (6\,GB) --- consumer hardware of
roughly signal-cabinet class. Control runs in SUMO on topologies built from
measured London data (Euston A501 with DfT hourly demand, a Bloomsbury grid,
Old Street junction), with a MaxPressure \cite{varaiya2013maxpressure} shield
and anti-starvation floor between the model and the lights, and every decision
bound into a hash-chained, signed audit record.

Contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A documented QLoRA$\to$int4$\to$Foundry-Local chain.} To our
knowledge the first published recipe for serving a custom fine-tuned model
through Foundry Local, including the registration and execution-provider
steps the toolchain does not document, with bit-identical decision
equivalence between CPU and WebGPU serving.
\item \textbf{A scale-saturation result.} Frontier distillation lifts a
0.6B student to 97--99\% holdout agreement with the teacher; a 3.8B student
lands on the same numbers. For this task class, capability is not the
bottleneck --- the distillation ceiling is.
\item \textbf{The authorship finding.} Offline accuracy decouples from
closed-loop delay (neither student significantly beats a fixed-time floor at
$n{=}30$), yet fine-tuning quadruples the fraction of decisions that survive
the shield. We introduce authored share as the metric a decision audit
needs, and show fine-tuning is what moves it.
\item \textbf{Honest nulls with mechanism.} We report the closed-loop nulls,
the one significant effect (repair of stock-model harm under congestion), and
a calibration argument for why the delay headroom was always small.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
LLMLight \cite{lai2023llmlight} distils GPT-4 rationales into LoRA-tuned
students down to 0.5B and remains the closest prior work; evaluation is on
CityFlow grids against cloud-served checkpoints. Traffic-R1
\cite{wang2025trafficr1} applies R1-style RL to a 3B model; CuraLight
\cite{curalight2026} is the one fine-tuning work on SUMO; DGLight
\cite{dglight2026} tunes an 8B model with GRPO and, usefully for calibration,
reports the \emph{entire} LLM-vs-MaxPressure gap at 1--2\%. iLLM-TSC
\cite{pang2024illmtsc} inverts our topology --- an RL agent proposes and a
cloud LLM vetoes --- which prices the veto path at cloud latency and
availability; our deterministic local verifier costs $O(\text{phases})$ per
decision. None of these serve on-device, none pair control with a
tamper-evident audit, and none evaluate on measured UK topologies. On the
safety side, our shield is deliberately weaker than correct-by-construction
shielding \cite{alshiekh2018shield}: it composes preemptive action masking
\cite{huang2020masking}, post-posed default substitution, and an
anti-starvation runtime monitor, and we discuss what a formal upgrade would
require.

\subsection{Control loop and shield}
Each junction is polled on a fixed decision interval ($\approx$10\,s
simulated). The SLM receives junction state in one of four prompt formats of
increasing privilege --- \emph{myopic} (per-phase halting counts),
\emph{sota} (delay-aware), \emph{prediction} (arrival forecasts),
\emph{coordination} (neighbour notes) --- and must return exactly
\texttt{\{"phase": N\}}. The shield masks the menu to conflict-free phases,
substitutes the MaxPressure phase when output is invalid, and enforces a
minimum-service floor. We define \textbf{override rate} as the fraction of
served SLM decisions that diverge from what MaxPressure would have chosen and
are \emph{not} served (the shield's choice prevails), and \textbf{authored
share} as the fraction of all actuations whose served phase originated with
the SLM. These are interference metrics in the sense of the shielding
literature, not veto counts.

\subsection{Audit}
Every decision emits a record (state digest, prompt hash, model proposal,
served action, serving authorship, latency) that is Ed25519-signed and
hash-chained per junction; chains verify end-to-end after each run. After a
simulated collision the system assembles a cited evidence pack against a UK
traffic-law knowledge base --- deliberately an evidence pack, not a verdict,
following the legal-hallucination risk analysis of \cite{dahl2024legal}.

\subsection{Distillation and training}
We sample 5{,}952 unique junction states from training-reserved seeds, label
them with a frontier teacher (cross-audited against a second frontier model:
98.0--99.0\% agreement), and render 59{,}533 training examples across the
four formats with family-correct chat templates and completion-only loss. A
hash-based split freezes a 5{,}580-example holdout before any training.
Students: Qwen3-0.6B and Phi-4-mini (3.8B), QLoRA $r{=}16$ NF4, merged to
bf16, compiled to int4 ONNX (RTN, weight-only) with the onnxruntime-genai
builder, and registered into Foundry Local. The 0.6B trains on the serving
GPU itself in 13.5\,h; the 3.8B on a single RTX~4090 in 2.3\,h. The stock
control for every comparison is the \emph{same} base model compiled through
the \emph{identical} int4 pipeline. CPU and WebGPU execution providers agree
bit-identically on holdout decisions (97.42\% both), so all sweeps run
GPU-served at sub-second p50.

\subsection{Closed-loop protocol}
Closed-loop cells pair the controller against a fixed-time baseline on the
same seed; evaluation seeds are disjoint from the four training-state seeds.
Per topology and arm: $n{=}30$ seeds, paired $t$ on mean network delay.

Three observations. First, the two fine-tuned students are statistically
indistinguishable despite a $6\times$ parameter gap, and both sit at the
teacher-agreement ceiling (the myopic ceiling is intrinsically lower: the
teacher was privileged with state the myopic prompt hides). Distillation
\emph{saturates} this task at 0.6B. Second, where the gain lands depends on
the base model: the 0.6B is lifted everywhere (+32 to +42 points), while
Phi-4-mini --- already 100\% format-compliant and 86--88\% accurate on three
formats stock --- gains substantially only on the delay-aware format
(+38.1 points). For a capable base model, fine-tuning buys decision quality,
not format discipline. Third, a leave-out-topology student (LOTO, trained
with no Old Street states) matches the generalist on every format: the
distilled policy does not memorise topology.

Neither arm significantly beats fixed-time on any topology; a $+42$-point
offline accuracy gain buys no significant delay change. The one significant
closed-loop effect of fine-tuning is protective: stock control significantly
\emph{harms} the congested grid ($+2.6\%$, $p{=}1.5\times10^{-3}$), the
fine-tuned model is neutral there, and the head-to-head paired difference is
$-1.8\%$ ($p{=}.019$). This decoupling has a calibration explanation: with
the shield bounding worst-case behaviour for both arms, and the total
LLM-vs-MaxPressure headroom reported at 1--2\% by work tuning models
$13\times$ larger \cite{dglight2026}, there was never much delay for
per-decision accuracy to win.
A Phi-4-mini confirmation arm (same 90 cells) lands arm-for-arm on the
0.6B student's numbers --- Euston $-3.8\%$ vs $-3.9\%$, Bloomsbury
$+0.6\%$ vs $+0.7\%$, override 0.12--0.17, authored share to 0.88 --- so
the saturation finding holds end-to-end, offline and closed-loop.

Forensics on the failure tail sharpens this into a stronger claim. Of 270
closed-loop cells, 14 are catastrophic ($\ge{+}20\%$ delay); all their
audit chains verify, and the catastrophic seeds are baseline-easy or
median realisations, so the failures are a controller property rather than
seed hardness. Critically, the two fine-tuned students share every one of
their catastrophic (topology, seed) pairs, with per-seed relative delay
matching to $\pm$0.2 points and near-identical decision compositions. Two
students of different architecture and $6\times$ different scale, trained
separately, fail identically --- evidence that distillation converges them
onto effectively the same policy. Saturation is not merely a shared
accuracy ceiling; it is a shared \emph{policy attractor}.

\subsection{Authorship: what fine-tuning actually moves}
The structural effect is unambiguous and consistent across every topology:
fine-tuning drops override from 0.46--0.60 to 0.11--0.18 and raises authored
share to 0.70--0.90 (stock: 0.54--0.68). The stock controller's delay numbers
are substantially the shield's delay numbers --- most of what it proposes is
replaced. The fine-tuned controller \emph{is} the controller.

This is the paper's claim: for a shielded, audited edge controller, the
deployable product of distillation is \textbf{authorship}. An accident audit
over a stock-model deployment attributes the served decisions to the
fallback; the same audit over the distilled deployment attributes them to the
model that the operator certified, versioned, and can retrain. Causal
accountability --- the property regulators and investigators need --- is
exactly what moved, and it moved by $4\times$.

\subsection{Authorship cuts both ways}
If fine-tuning makes the model author the decisions, it also makes the
model's inputs worth attacking. We ran an adversarial trust battery
(honest / blatant / stealth neighbour misreporting) against stock and
fine-tuned controllers at both scales. Detection is controller-independent
by design and in measurement --- the ledger scores neighbour claims against
local sensing, and a blatant liar is caught and collapsed under every
controller (trust $\le$0.27, corroboration power stripped) while a
calibrated stealth attacker evades everywhere (trust $\ge$0.92). But the
delay cost of a blatant attack, relative to each controller's own honest
run, is \emph{larger} for the fine-tuned arms on synthetic grids ($+9$ to
$+11$ points) than for stock ($+3$ to $+7$): a controller that genuinely
acts on its coordination inputs is moved further by poisoned ones. The
trust ledger is therefore a necessary companion to distillation, not an
optional extra --- and stealth misreporting, cheap and undetected, is the
open problem.

We also measured a specialisation cost. On an adjacent structured task
(vehicle-authorisation classification against a curated rule base), the
fine-tuned students perform far \emph{worse} than their own stock bases:
the 0.6B student cannot emit the task's \texttt{\{"class","grant"\}}
schema at all without in-prompt scaffolding (26/26 parse failures),
having been captured onto \texttt{\{"phase": N\}}. A 200-item MMLU probe
shows only mild general-capability decline (57.5\% $\to$ 53.0\%,
$p{=}.37$), so near-neighbour structured tasks are where the cost
concentrates. This is an argument for architectural separation, not
against distillation: the deterministic gate should keep authorisation,
and the distilled model should keep phase selection.

\subsection{Serving reliability at the edge}
Sustained serving on consumer hardware is itself a finding: across the
campaign we recorded four Foundry Local reliability events (garbage-token
emission after long uptime; two unprompted service self-restarts mid-run;
registration requiring undocumented synthesis of the inference manifest for
builder-compiled models). All were mitigated in harness (endpoint
rediscovery, reload-and-retry with intact statistics, fail-loud parse
accounting), and p50 decision latency held at 0.4--1.1\,s GPU-served ---
comfortably inside the 10\,s decision interval with a $9\times$ margin.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
Simulation-only, three topologies, one city. Neither student
significantly beats the fixed-time floor, and the trust mechanism does not
stop a stealth attacker. We distil labels, not reasoning traces --- Orca-style trace distillation \cite{mukherjee2023orca} might
transfer more; our myopic format shows the cost of privileged teachers
directly. The shield is a heuristic composite, not a synthesized shield with
correctness guarantees \cite{alshiekh2018shield}. ``Lossless'' int4 is scoped
strictly as bit-identical holdout accuracy, weight-only RTN. A seeded
200-item MMLU retention probe through the same endpoints finds no
significant general-capability loss at 3.8B (57.5\% stock vs 53.0\%
fine-tuned, $p{=}.37$); at 0.6B the stock model already scores at chance
on this format, so forgetting is unmeasurable there by floor effect.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A 0.6B SLM, distilled from a frontier teacher for 13 GPU-hours on the same
6\,GB device that serves it, reaches the teacher-agreement ceiling; six times
the parameters add nothing. Closed-loop it does not beat a fixed-time floor
--- and neither does anything else within the shield's envelope --- but it
stops needing the shield: override falls $4\times$, authored share reaches
0.90, and the stock model's one significant harm disappears. For edge traffic
control, where every actuation must survive an audit, that conversion ---
from shield-driven to model-authored control --- is the contribution
fine-tuning makes. Future work: GRPO-style closed-loop tuning
\cite{dglight2026}, formal shield synthesis, and a hardware-in-the-loop
pilot.
