%==================== SCOPE ====================
\section{1.\ The project, and where it is headed}
Picture a row of traffic junctions. Each one is run by a small AI model (\textbf{Phi-4-mini}) on the junction itself, through \textbf{Microsoft Foundry Local}, never retrained. The junctions tell each other what they are about to do, over a channel that is digitally signed and sanity-checked. Underneath sits a fast classical controller (\emph{MaxPressure}$^{[1]}$). It is the default, and it is the safety floor.

The goal is simple to state. Coordinate well in normal traffic, and \textbf{fail gracefully when a neighbour lies}. If a junction's messages are tampered with, or the junction itself is hijacked, the system should notice and fall back safely instead of believing it. A deterministic safety net always makes the clear-cut safety calls: clear an ambulance it can actually see, and refuse a claim it cannot verify. Safety never depends on the AI. \textbf{The AI handles only the hard cases a fixed rule cannot settle.} When an alert is flagged but the evidence is mixed, it weighs what the neighbours report and judges: real incident, or faked grab? Whether that on-device reasoning beats a well-tuned fixed rule is the central question we measure. We will report the answer honestly, even if the rule turns out to be enough.

%==================== PROGRESS ====================
\vspace{1pt}
\section{4.\ What we have done}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{itemize}
\item \hd{Built and tested:} signed messaging between junctions, an approved-senders list, the classical controller with its safety net, a check that flags neighbour reports that do not match the real traffic, and a four-junction simulated road.
\item \hd{Emergency handling:} a junction clears an ambulance only when it has real proof the vehicle exists. A valid signature is not enough. So a faked emergency is refused.
\item \hd{Live attack-and-defence demo:} a hijacked junction sends a signed but fake ``ambulance coming'' alert. It is refused. A real ambulance is cleared at every junction. We compare against a trust-everything system and the plain classical controller.
\item \hd{Evidence (30 runs):} emergency handling clears the ambulance \textbf{31\,s faster} than having none. The defence blocks an attack that would otherwise add \textbf{16\,s} to the worst side-street wait. Demanding proof costs \textbf{8\,s} versus blindly trusting; that cost is deliberate.
\item \hd{Independent check:} an automated audit of our written spec found \emph{no made-up sources}. A few over-stated citations were fixed. Every result can be reproduced from its settings.
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}

%==================== NOVELTY ====================
\vspace{-2pt}
\section{5.\ Why it is new}
No one has tested a setup like this: many junctions, coordinated by AI, where a junction's messages can be \emph{faked by an insider that still holds a valid key}. The parts exist separately. The combination does not. Closest work: \textbf{CoLLMLight}$^{[5]}$ (AI junctions that coordinate, but trust each other completely); \textbf{VLMLight}$^{[6]}$ (fast-classical plus slow-AI, one junction, very large models, no trust); \textbf{LA-Light}$^{[7]}$ (AI for rare events, cloud-based, no malicious faults); \textbf{Keijzer\,2021}$^{[8]}$ (detects coordination attacks, but only in theory). Our difference: a small on-device AI, a \emph{measured} picture of how detection holds up as the attack grows, verifiable identity, and a tamper-proof log.

%==================== NEXT ====================
\section{6.\ Next steps}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{itemize}
\item \hd{The central experiment:} on flagged, unclear cases, does the AI actually beat a well-tuned fixed rule? We measure this and report it either way.
\item Bigger evaluation: more traffic levels and a real London (Lambeth) road, with many repeated runs and error ranges.
\item Find the breaking point: grow the lie until detection fails, and report where.
\item Heavier traffic, where the ambulance benefit and the attack damage are largest.
\item Run the real Phi-4-mini on Foundry Local and confirm it answers consistently.
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}

%==================== HONESTY ====================
\vspace{-1pt}
\section{7.\ What we do and do not claim}
{\footnotesize \textbf{We claim:} trustworthy messaging between junctions; reliable detection of faked or faulty reports, with a measured reliability curve; a guaranteed safety floor; and a fair attack-and-defence test. \textbf{We do not claim:} new cryptography; that the AI beats the classical controller on ordinary traffic; that the AI already improves the hard-case decisions (we measure that next); defence against two or more junctions working together, or a stolen master key; or that simulator results carry straight over to real hardware (future work).}
