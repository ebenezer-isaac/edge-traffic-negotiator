# Adversarial-Failure Analysis of AI/LLM and Coordinated Traffic-Signal-Control Systems: A Critical Synthesis for "The Edge Negotiator"

## TL;DR
- Across every LLM/DRL/coordinated TSC system examined, the controller **implicitly trusts every input it ingests** — neighbour reports, vehicle messages, or emergency-vehicle signals are consumed as ground truth, and not one of the ten primary systems models an authenticated-but-lying insider in the coordination layer; the closest defensive work (blockchain, Sybil-resilience, anomaly detection) defends the *vehicle-to-infrastructure* channel or *external* attackers, not a trusted coordinating node.
- The research gap "The Edge Negotiator" targets is genuine and precisely locatable: **no existing system simultaneously (i) coordinates multiple junctions, (ii) uses an edge language model for exception handling, and (iii) is explicitly robust to a compromised-but-authenticated coordination peer** — CoLLMLight coordinates but has zero input verification; REG-TSC/VLMLight handle emergencies with an LLM but are per-junction and trust the EV signal; the security literature attacks or defends the vehicle channel but assumes signal controllers/coordination peers are honest.
- Emergency-vehicle preemption is the highest-leverage attack surface because a single message carries outsized physical authority (forced greens, cleared lanes); combined with the documented "last-vehicle advantage" (a single spoofing vehicle raised I-SIG delay by up to 68.1%, reversing its 26.6% benefit) and the colluding-vehicle results, a lying insider becomes physically consequential — motivating a *frozen, inspectable* edge SLM that cannot be poisoned via online learning.

## Key Findings

1. **Universal implicit trust.** The colluding-vehicles attack paper states the failure mode bluntly: "The DRL models fully 'trust' that vehicles are sending the true information to the signals, making the ATCS vulnerable to adversarial attacks with falsified information" (Qu, Tang & Ma, "Attacking Deep Reinforcement Learning-Based Traffic Signal Control Systems with Colluding Vehicles," arXiv:2111.02845; ACM TIST 14(6), DOI 10.1145/3625236, 2023). This trust posture recurs in every coordinated/LLM system reviewed.

2. **Coordination = attack surface.** Systems that ingest neighbour data (CoLight, CoLLMLight, EMVLight, Max-Pressure) expand the attack surface: each neighbour message is an unauthenticated write into the local decision function. CoLLMLight even encodes trust-by-default in its prompt: "If the upstream or downstream information of X isn't mentioned, it means that they are in a good state with low occupancy" — so *withholding* a report is itself an exploit.

3. **Preemption amplifies risk.** EMVLight, VLMLight and REG-TSC give an emergency-vehicle indication the authority to force phase changes network-wide. None authenticate the EV signal; REG-TSC ingests the EV's "planned route, its current position, and its current speed" as ground truth.

4. **Defenses target the wrong adversary.** The mature security work (Chen et al. NDSS'18; Feng et al. TRR 2018; blockchain arXiv:1906.02628; Sybil-resilience; Keijzer et al. arXiv:2104.03801) addresses *external* spoofers or *vehicle-side* falsification — not an authenticated coordination peer that is trusted by construction.

5. **A frozen edge SLM is a defensible assurance choice** precisely because trained-RL systems (CoLight, SafeLight, LightGPT-as-trained) require retraining under distribution shift and are exposed to online-learning/data-poisoning, whereas a frozen model's decision policy is fixed, inspectable, and cannot be silently mutated by malicious inputs.

6. **Citation hygiene flag:** arXiv:2007.08041 does **not** resolve to a DRL/adaptive-TSC attack survey — see Caveats.

## Details

### PART A — Per-System Analysis

#### A.1 CoLLMLight (arXiv:2503.11739, Yuan, Lai & Liu, 2025)
- **(1) Inputs/from whom.** Each intersection agent ingests, for every connected lane of *itself and its neighbouring intersections*, the vector o_t = [n_queue, n_move, τ, ρ] (queue length, moving-vehicle count, average waiting time, lane occupancy). It builds a directed spatiotemporal subgraph 𝒢 = (𝒱, ℒ) over assigned + neighbour intersections, plus temporal history 𝒯_t of past observations and signal configurations at both the assigned and neighbouring intersections. Neighbour data arrives as literal prompt table rows ("Nearby Upstream and Downstream Lanes – Controlled by Other Intersections near You"). A communication threshold α (=0.5) filters which neighbour lanes are aggregated: "only neighboring lanes with occupancy above the threshold α are aggregated."
- **(2) Trust assumption (verified by full-text reading).** Implicit and total. There is no trust/verification/authentication mechanism anywhere in the paper; the words "trust", "spoofing", "malicious", "compromised" do not appear in relation to neighbour data. The only "robustness" claimed is generalization across traffic scenarios ("demonstrating its robustness across different traffic scenarios"). The paper's only reliability-type concern is the model's *own* reasoning: "its decision-making remains suboptimal due to potential hallucinations in traffic conditions and misinterpretations of spatiotemporal dependencies." An explicit trust-by-default statement appears in the prompt: "If the upstream or downstream information of X isn't mentioned, it means that they are in a good state with low occupancy."
- **(3) Failure mode under a single lying insider.** A single authenticated neighbour junction inflates its reported queue lengths/occupancy (or fabricates near-saturation on lanes feeding the target). The target LLM agent, reasoning over the poisoned spatiotemporal graph, predicts congestion propagation that does not exist and extends/gives green to the compromised approach, starving the competing (honest) approach. Conversely, a neighbour that *withholds* its report triggers the "good state with low occupancy" default, causing the target to under-serve a genuinely congested upstream lane → queue spillback and local gridlock.
- **(4) Any check/limit.** None. There is no plausibility check, no cross-validation of neighbour reports against local sensing, no authentication, and no adversarial/faulty/compromised-neighbour scenario is discussed at all. It misses every insider attack by construction.

#### A.2 VLMLight (arXiv:2505.19486, Wang, Chen, Pang, Cai, Chen, Kan & Pun, 2025)
- **(1) Inputs/from whom.** Multi-view *images* at the intersection (first image-based TSC simulator, TransSimHub), from which a VLM reads vehicle type, motion, and spatial density; a fast RL policy handles routine traffic and an LLM meta-controller routes safety-critical cases to a multi-agent reasoning branch that prioritizes emergency vehicles and verifies rule compliance.
- **(2) Trust assumption.** The visual perception pipeline is trusted; the paper frames the LLM as "safety-prioritized meta-controller" but assumes the image inputs and detected EV are genuine. No adversarial-perception or spoofed-EV threat model. (Inferred from the input pipeline and the absence of a threat model.)
- **(3) Failure mode.** A spoofed emergency vehicle in view (or an adversarial visual pattern) causes the meta-controller to divert to the emergency-reasoning branch and grant preemptive green, clearing lanes for a non-existent EV — unsafe preemption and deliberate green-time theft. The paper's own headline benefit is exactly the lever an attacker hijacks: "VLMLight reduces waiting times for emergency vehicles by up to 65% over RL-only systems, while preserving real-time performance in standard conditions with less than 1% degradation" (e.g., average EV waiting time from 22.0 s to 7.48 s in the Songdo network), with full inference "within 11.5 seconds," fitting the typical 13 s signal-phase buffer.
- **(4) Any check/limit.** It has an LLM "rule-compliance verification" step — but this checks *traffic-rule legality of the phase*, not the *authenticity of the EV or the image*. It misses input spoofing entirely.

#### A.3 LA-Light / LLM-Assisted Light (arXiv:2403.08337, Wang, Pang, Kan, Pun, Chen & Huang, 2024)
- **(1) Inputs/from whom.** A hybrid framework augmenting an LLM with perception/decision "tools" that query static and dynamic traffic information at a single intersection; explicitly tested under Sensor Outage (SO).
- **(2) Trust assumption.** Tool outputs are trusted; the system is designed for *degraded/missing* data, not *maliciously falsified* data. Robustness is framed as handling sensor outage, not adversarial inputs. (Inferred.)
- **(3) Failure mode.** Because the system is built to reason around missing sensor data, an insider that supplies *plausible but false* tool readings (rather than dropping them) evades the outage-handling logic: the LLM treats the fabricated reading as valid context and issues a phase decision favouring the attacker's approach.
- **(4) Any check/limit.** Handles Sensor Outage — a graceful-degradation mechanism for *absent* data: "in cases of Sensor Outage (SO), our approach surpasses conventional RL-based systems by reducing the average waiting time by 20.4%" (and a 32.1% ATT reduction versus Max-Pressure at the four-way intersection). Limit: it has no mechanism to detect *false-but-present* data.

#### A.4 iLLM-TSC (arXiv:2407.06025, Pang, Wang, Pun, Chen & Xiong, 2024)
- **(1) Inputs/from whom.** Two-stage: an RL agent decides from observed traffic data; an LLM then evaluates the decision using additional real-time context (e.g., presence of emergency vehicles), correcting "unreasonable" RL actions. Explicitly targets imperfect observations from degraded communication — packet loss, delay, noise.
- **(2) Trust assumption.** The LLM's contextual inputs (including the EV presence signal) are trusted. Degradation is modelled as *stochastic communication faults*, not adversarial manipulation. (Inferred.)
- **(3) Failure mode.** An insider injects a false "emergency vehicle present" context. The LLM overrides a correct RL decision and grants preemption; because the LLM is positioned as the arbiter of "reasonableness," its override authority is exactly what the false context captures → unnecessary preemption, disruption of the honest phase.
- **(4) Any check/limit.** The LLM's "reasonableness" check on RL actions is a consistency mechanism — but it validates the *RL action against context*, trusting the *context itself*. Reported benefit: "In scenarios with degraded communication, iLLM-TSC reduces the average waiting time by 17.5% compared to traditional RL methods" (and reduces EV waiting time by 62.9% vs SARL-TSC). No defense against falsified context.

#### A.5 LLMLight / LightGPT (arXiv:2312.16044, Lai, Xu, Zhang, Liu & Xiong, 2024)
- **(1) Inputs/from whom.** A single-intersection agent receives a "knowledgeable prompt detailing real-time traffic conditions" (lane-level queue/waiting states) and reasons via chain-of-thought; LightGPT is a fine-tuned backbone.
- **(2) Trust assumption.** The textualized traffic state is trusted as accurate. No neighbour coordination in the base design and no input-integrity model. (Inferred.)
- **(3) Failure mode.** A compromised sensor/feeder inflates the reported queue on one movement; LLMLight, reasoning "human-like" over the prompt, allocates green to the inflated approach, starving competitors. As a single-junction controller its blast radius is local, but the trust posture is identical.
- **(4) Any check/limit.** None specific to input integrity; interpretability (human-readable reasoning) is an *audit* aid post hoc, not a runtime plausibility check.

#### A.6 EMVLight (arXiv:2206.13441, Su, Zhong, Chow, Dey & Jin, 2022; Transportation Research Part C)
- **(1) Inputs/from whom.** Decentralized multi-agent advantage actor-critic: multi-class RL agents ingest network state including the EV's presence/location for joint dynamic routing + signal preemption, with a pressure-based reward and policy sharing.
- **(2) Trust assumption.** The EV location/route signal driving preemption is trusted implicitly; the framework's purpose is to *act on* the EV signal, not to authenticate it. (Inferred.)
- **(3) Failure mode.** A spoofed EV location causes cascading preemption: EMVLight forms a "green wave" and clears lanes along a fabricated route, imposing large disturbances on non-EV traffic — the paper itself notes that traffic-signal pre-emption practices "usually lead to significant disturbances in traffic flow and subsequently increase the travel time for non-EMVs." One false message thus commands network-level physical action. (Legitimate benefit reported: up to 42.6% EMV travel-time reduction and 23.5% shorter average travel time.)
- **(4) Any check/limit.** None; preemption authority is delegated to the (unauthenticated) EV signal. This is the canonical "preemption amplifies risk" case.

#### A.7 REG-TSC (arXiv:2510.26242, Li et al., 2025; IEEE)
- **(1) Inputs/from whom.** Distributed *per-intersection* LLM agents; observation = intersection topology, action space, queuing vehicles, approaching vehicles. **No neighbour message exchange.** Emergency-vehicle state Ev_t = "its planned route, its current position, and its current speed", ingested as ground truth; deep reasoning triggers "only when an emergency vehicle is either currently at intersection i, or when intersection i appears in the planned path of the emergency vehicle." A Reviewer-based Emergency RAG (RERAG) retrieves historical guidance.
- **(2) Trust assumption (verified by full-text reading).** The EV route/position/speed is trusted as accurate; no authentication. "Verification" in the paper refers only to vetting *retrieved knowledge* to curb LLM hallucination ("existing RAG merely retrieve raw information without effective verification or distillation"), not verifying live inputs. Its reliability language ("rational and reliable strategies") targets model hallucination, not input integrity.
- **(3) Failure mode.** A spoofed EV route that lists the target intersection triggers REG-TSC's emergency deep-reasoning branch and preemptive service for a phantom ambulance → unsafe preemption and green-time diversion; RAG guidance amplifies the confident-but-wrong action.
- **(4) Any check/limit.** RERAG mitigates *hallucination* (model-side error), not *input spoofing* (data-side attack). No adversarial/spoofed-EV scenario is considered.

#### A.8 CoLight (arXiv:1905.05717, Wei, Xu, Zhang, Zheng, Zang, Chen, Zhang, Zhu, Xu & Li, CIKM 2019)
- **(1) Inputs/from whom.** Graph attention network where each intersection agent incorporates the observed states (queue/vehicle counts) of neighbouring intersections via learned attention; "index-free" neighbour modelling.
- **(2) Trust assumption.** Neighbour observations are trusted inputs to the attention mechanism; the attention weights model *influence*, not *trustworthiness*. (Inferred.)
- **(3) Failure mode.** A compromised neighbour feeding falsified state is attended to by the GAT as if genuine; the paper documents that agents learn to rely more on informative upstream neighbours ("Inter 9-49 relies more on the upstream intersections and less on the downstream"), so a false signal from a high-attention neighbour is *weighted up*, not filtered — misallocated green, starvation of competing approaches.
- **(4) Any check/limit.** None. Attention is a performance mechanism, not a defense; a high learned attention weight on a malicious neighbour is a liability, not a safeguard.

#### A.9 SafeLight (arXiv:2211.10871, Du, Ye, Gu, Li, Wei & Wang, AAAI 2023)
- **(1) Inputs/from whom.** Single-intersection safety-enhanced residual RL; ingests traffic state and enforces road-safety rules (a safety model deciding if a TSC action is unsafe) via multi-objective loss and reward shaping.
- **(2) Trust assumption.** The traffic-state input used to evaluate the safety model is trusted; safety is defined against *physical collision rules*, not *input authenticity*. (Inferred.)
- **(3) Failure mode.** The safety layer constrains *actions* (e.g., preventing conflicting greens) but if inputs are falsified, SafeLight still optimizes delay on false premises; an insider inflating one approach shifts green while staying within collision-safety constraints → degraded efficiency/starvation without triggering the safety model.
- **(4) Any check/limit.** Yes — an explicit safety-rule enforcement layer (toward zero collisions). Limit: it guards against *unsafe phase transitions*, not against *false state inputs*; a lying insider can steer decisions while every action remains "collision-safe."

#### A.10 Max-Pressure control (Varaiya, "Max pressure control of a network of signalized intersections," Transportation Research Part C: Emerging Technologies, 36:177–195, 2013, DOI 10.1016/j.trc.2013.08.014)
- **(1) Inputs/from whom.** Each intersection computes "pressure" per phase from queue lengths on its incoming lanes and the queues on connected *downstream* (neighbour) lanes; it selects the max-pressure phase using only local + immediate-neighbour queue measurements. Provably throughput-optimal / stabilizes queues under a store-and-forward model.
- **(2) Trust assumption.** Explicit dependence on *accurate queue measurements*; the stability proof rests on idealizations (infinite link capacity, zero link travel time, no switching loss) and true state. Correctness is contingent on honest queue data.
- **(3) Failure mode.** An insider reporting inflated downstream queue (or deflated incoming queue) directly manipulates the pressure computation: the controller deterministically switches to the phase the attacker wants, because the decision *is* a function of the reported queues. No learning needed — the manipulation is analytic and immediate.
- **(4) Any check/limit.** None; it is a transparent deterministic control law with no plausibility filter. Its transparency is a double-edged trait: fully inspectable, but trivially gamed if inputs are corrupted.

### PART A (Security-side sources)

#### S.1 Colluding-vehicles attack (arXiv:2111.02845; ACM TIST 14(6), DOI 10.1145/3625236, Qu, Tang & Ma, 2023)
- **Attacked:** DRL-based ATCS that collect state from nearby vehicles. **Trust broken:** "The DRL models fully 'trust' that vehicles are sending the true information to the signals." **Failure mode:** COLLUSIONVEH, a DRL-based framework (road-situation encoder + vehicle interpreter + communication module), coordinates a *group* of vehicles to send falsified presence information to a *black-box* DRL controller, reducing the colluders' total travel time; the colluding effect *decreases* as the number of colluders grows. **Defense limit:** the paper only demonstrates the attack and *suggests* detection/filtering of falsified data as future work — no defense is implemented. This is the closest analogue to an insider, but the adversary is a *vehicle* (external data source), not a trusted coordination peer.

#### S.2 UCI/TRB I-SIG vulnerability studies
- Two linked primary sources: **Chen, Yin, Feng, Mao & Liu, "Exposing Congestion Attack on Emerging Connected Vehicle based Traffic Signal Control," NDSS 2018** (Qi Alfred Chen et al., UC Irvine / U. Michigan), and **Feng, Huang, Chen, Liu & Mao, "Vulnerability of Traffic Control System Under Cyberattacks with Falsified Data," Transportation Research Record 2672(1):1–11, 2018** (the TRB venue).
- **Attacked:** I-SIG, the USDOT-approved CV-based signal control pilot using Basic Safety Messages over DSRC. **Trust broken:** the controller trusts vehicle-broadcast trajectory/BSM data. **Failure mode:** a *single* spoofing vehicle exploits the "last vehicle advantage, meaning that the latest arriving vehicle can determine the signal plan," increasing total delay "as high as 68.1%, which completely reverses the benefit of using the I-SIG system (26.6% decrease) and cause[s] the mobility to be even 23.4% worse than that without using the I-SIG system" (a two-stage variant reaches "over 60.6% in the total delay"). Root cause: a security-vs-deployability trade-off forcing a less-optimal algorithm configuration on compute-limited RSUs. **Defense limit:** authors recommend robust transition-period algorithms, better RSU compute, and infrastructure-controlled sensor-based spoofing detection — mitigations against *external* spoofers, still assuming the controller/infrastructure is honest.

#### S.3 Blockchain-based TSC (arXiv:1906.02628, Li, Nejad & Zhang, IEEE ICIOT 2019)
- **Title:** "A Blockchain-Based Architecture for Traffic Signal Control Systems." **Security property provided:** decentralized, "secure-by-design" data integrity/authenticity for CV data, applied to defend I-SIG against congestion attacks in a simulation study. **Limit:** it authenticates and secures *vehicle data provenance* on a distributed ledger; it raises the bar for *unauthenticated/external* spoofing but does *not* stop an *authenticated* node that is entitled to write yet lies — precisely the insider threat. Blockchain gives non-repudiation, not truthfulness.

#### S.4 Keijzer, Jarmolowitz & Ferrari (arXiv:2104.03801, 2021)
- **Exact title:** "Detection of Cyber-Attacks in Collaborative Intersection Control." **What is attacked:** signage-less cooperative-AV intersection control over wireless V2V/V2I. **Trust broken:** vehicles' communicated data (false data injection). **Mechanism/failure mode addressed:** a Sliding-Mode-Observer-based detector estimates and detects false-data-injection attacks on vehicle communications, with a novel detection logic improving detection performance. **Limit:** model-based observer detection assumes a known plant model and a specific FDI attack structure; a stealthy/model-consistent insider that stays within observer residual thresholds can evade detection — the classic stealthy-attack limit of residual-based detectors in cyber-physical systems.

#### S.5 Survey (arXiv:2007.08041) — DISCREPANCY, see Caveats.
- This ID resolves to a **survey of security attacks and defenses on Connected and Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs)** (184 papers, 2000–2020), *not* a DRL/adaptive-TSC-specific attack survey. It is relevant for a CAV attack taxonomy (attack models by targeted component, access requirements, and attack motives) but has been mis-labelled in the brief. The genuine DRL-TSC-attack primary source is arXiv:2111.02845 (S.1).

### PART B — Synthesis

**(a) Coordinating on neighbour reports is a growing, physically-consequential attack surface.** From first principles: a coordinated controller's decision is a function d = f(local_state, neighbour_reports). Every neighbour report is an *unauthenticated write* into f. As TSC moves from isolated actuation (Max-Pressure at one junction) to network-wide learned coordination (CoLight's attention over neighbours; CoLLMLight's spatiotemporal graph), the number of such writes — and the *weight* each carries in f — grows. Three properties make this dangerous: (i) **physical actuation** — unlike a recommender, a TSC decision moves real vehicles, so a corrupted input produces kinetic consequences (spillback, gridlock, or collision exposure); (ii) **feedback coupling** — falsified upstream state changes downstream flow, which changes future reports, so errors propagate (documented as congestion propagation in CoLLMLight and as network disturbance in EMVLight); (iii) **learned reliance** — attention/graph models *learn to trust* the most informative neighbours, so an insider on a high-influence node has amplified leverage (CoLight explicitly learns higher reliance on upstream neighbours). The colluding-vehicle result (S.1) and the last-vehicle-advantage result (S.2, up to 68.1% delay increase from one vehicle) are empirical proof that small, well-placed false inputs yield large physical effects.

**Emergency-vehicle preemption amplifies this risk** because it collapses the many-inputs-averaged decision into a *single-message override*: one "EV present / EV route" assertion is designed to seize network authority — force greens, clear lanes, form green waves (EMVLight, VLMLight, REG-TSC). Preemption thus concentrates maximal physical authority into the *least-verified* message class. An authenticated-but-lying insider that asserts a phantom EV gets deterministic, high-priority physical action with no plausibility gate in any reviewed system. The very feature that saves lives (VLMLight's up-to-65% / 22.0 s → 7.48 s EV-wait reduction; EMVLight's 42.6% EMV travel-time cut) is the feature that, unauthenticated, becomes a precision denial-of-service and safety hazard.

**(b) The precise unoccupied research gap.** No existing system satisfies all three of: (i) multi-junction coordination, (ii) an edge language model for exception handling, and (iii) explicit robustness to an authenticated-but-lying insider in the coordination layer. Each near-neighbour fails on exactly one axis:
- **CoLLMLight** — (i)✓ coordinates, (ii)✓ LLM, but (iii)✗ zero input verification and explicit trust-by-default; no threat model.
- **CoLight / Max-Pressure** — (i)✓ coordinate, (iii)✗ trust neighbour queues, and (ii)✗ no language-model exception handling.
- **REG-TSC / VLMLight / iLLM-TSC / LA-Light / LLMLight** — (ii)✓ use an LLM/VLM for exceptions, but (i)✗ are per-junction (REG-TSC has *no* neighbour exchange) and (iii)✗ trust the EV/context signal.
- **EMVLight** — (i)✓ coordinates preemption, but (ii)✗ RL not language model, and (iii)✗ trusts the EV signal.
- **SafeLight** — has a safety layer but it guards *action legality*, not *input integrity*, and is single-junction (i)✗ (ii)✗.
- **Security work (S.1–S.4)** — models the adversary, but as an *external/vehicle-side* spoofer (colluding vehicles, BSM spoofing, FDI on V2X) or defends *data provenance* (blockchain); none treats a *trusted coordination peer* that authenticates correctly yet emits false coordination data. The blockchain work (S.3) is the sharpest illustration: it guarantees *who* sent a message, not *whether the message is true* — orthogonal to the insider-lying threat.

The intersection of all three — a coordinated, multi-junction system that uses an edge SLM specifically to *arbitrate exceptions from potentially-lying peers* — is empty. That is "The Edge Negotiator's" contribution.

**(c) Why a frozen edge SLM (no retraining) is defensible for exception handling.** Trained-RL and fine-tuned approaches (CoLight, SafeLight, LightGPT-as-trained, EMVLight) must be *retrained* to accommodate novel situations or distribution shift, and any system with an online/continual-learning path exposes a *data-poisoning* surface: a lying insider that persists can shift the learned policy itself, converting a transient attack into a permanent backdoor. A frozen SLM removes that surface entirely — its decision policy is fixed at deployment, so no stream of malicious coordination messages can mutate its parameters. Equally important for a safety case, a frozen model is *inspectable and version-pinned*: its behaviour on a fixed adversarial test battery is reproducible, its exception-handling logic can be argued in an assurance case, and its outputs are auditable against a stable specification rather than a moving target. Freezing therefore trades marginal adaptivity for two security/assurance advantages the trained baselines cannot offer: immunity to online poisoning and a stationary, certifiable decision function.

### PART C — Metrics and Assurance

**(i) Resilience / graceful-degradation metrics for cyber-physical systems.** The canonical framing measures performance as a time function around a disruption: **degradation rate** (slope of performance loss after attack onset), **robustness/residual performance** (minimum or steady-state performance during attack), **recovery time** (time to return to acceptable performance), and **recovery/steady-state performance after attack** — the "resilience triangle"–style measures standard in CPS resilience engineering. For RL specifically, Pullum, "Review of metrics to measure the stability, robustness and resilience of reinforcement learning" (arXiv:2203.12048, 2022) catalogues stability/robustness/resilience metrics for RL agents. In the TSC-under-disruption literature, robustness is operationalized via performance drop under demand surges, incidents, and sensor failures (Rodrigues & Azevedo, ITSC 2019; comparative robustness studies such as "Robustness of Reinforcement Learning-Based Traffic Signal Control under Incidents," arXiv:2506.13836, 2025).

**(ii) Fairness metrics in TSC evaluation.**
- **Jain's fairness index** J = (Σ w_n)² / (N Σ w_n²), ranging 1/N (worst) to 1 (best): applied to per-vehicle waiting times in fair-TSC RL work — e.g., "A Deep Reinforcement Learning Approach for Fair Traffic Signal Control" (arXiv:2107.10146), which constructs a delay-based fairness controller DFC_α whose fairness term is explicitly analysed via Jain's index.
- **Gini coefficient** (egalitarian fairness of the delay distribution), **Rawlsian max-delay** (worst-case/maximum delay), and utilitarian total travel time are used together in recent fairness-focused signal work — e.g., "FairSCOSCA: Fairness At Arterial Signals" (arXiv:2601.06275), which maps Gini→egalitarian, max-delay→Rawlsian, average delay→Harsanyian, total travel time→utilitarian. Delay-based throughput-and-fairness trade-offs at isolated intersections are treated by Wu, Ghosal, Zhang & Chuah, "Delay-based traffic signal control for throughput optimality and fairness at an isolated intersection," IEEE Trans. Vehicular Technology 67:896–909, 2018.

**(iii) Safety assurance cases and GSN for ML-in-the-loop control.**
- **Goal Structuring Notation (GSN)** — graphical safety-argument notation with core elements Goal (claim), Strategy (inference), Solution (evidence), Context, Assumption, Justification; originated at the University of York in the early 1990s (Kelly; Kelly & Weaver, "The Goal Structuring Notation – a safety argument notation"), now maintained as the community **GSN Standard (v3)** by the SCSC Assurance Case Working Group.
- **AMLAS** — "Assurance of Machine Learning for use in Autonomous Systems," a six-stage GSN-based ML assurance process (Hawkins, Paterson, Picardi, Jia, Calinescu & Habli, University of York) — the key framework for arguing ML-component safety.
- **SACM** — the OMG Structured Assurance Case Metamodel, the model-based successor generalizing GSN/CAE (see "Model Based System Assurance Using the Structured Assurance Case Metamodel," arXiv:1905.02427; and, on GSN-centric engineering, "ACCESS: Assurance Case Centric Engineering of Safety-critical Systems," arXiv:2403.15236). These are the tools "The Edge Negotiator" should use to argue that a frozen SLM's insider-robustness claim is supported by adversarial-test evidence.

## Recommendations
1. **Stage 1 — Adopt the insider threat model explicitly.** Formally define the authenticated-but-lying insider (a node with valid credentials that emits arbitrary false coordination/EV data) and position it against the reviewed systems, all of which assume honest inputs. Use CoLLMLight (coordination without verification) and REG-TSC/EMVLight (preemption without EV authentication) as the primary "vulnerable baselines."
2. **Stage 2 — Build a plausibility/consistency layer, since none of the ten systems has one.** The SLM should reconcile each neighbour report against locally-sensed state and physical conservation (flow continuity between adjacent links) — turning Max-Pressure's transparency into a *sanity constraint* rather than a vulnerability. Trigger to quarantine a peer: a neighbour's reported queue is inconsistent with local downstream arrivals beyond a physically-plausible bound.
3. **Stage 3 — Treat preemption as privileged.** Require multi-source corroboration before granting EV preemption (never a single message), because preemption concentrates maximal physical authority. Design rule: any single-message path to a forced green is a defect (cf. the last-vehicle advantage and phantom-EV failure modes).
4. **Stage 4 — Evaluate with resilience + fairness metrics, not just average delay.** Report degradation rate, residual performance, and recovery time under a scripted insider attack, plus Jain's index and Rawlsian max-delay to show the compromised approach's competitor is not starved. Acceptance threshold for "graceful degradation": bounded, recoverable performance loss with finite max-delay (no starvation).
5. **Stage 5 — Author a GSN/AMLAS assurance case** arguing the frozen-SLM insider-robustness claim, using the adversarial test battery as Solutions/evidence. This is where the frozen, inspectable model pays off: a stationary decision function admits a reproducible safety argument that a continually-retrained RL policy cannot.
6. **Do not rely on authentication/blockchain alone.** Because provenance ≠ truthfulness, credential-based and ledger-based defenses (S.3) are necessary but insufficient against the insider; the SLM's plausibility reasoning is the load-bearing defense.

- **arXiv:2007.08041 mismatch (flagged):** this ID resolves to a Connected-and-Autonomous-Vehicle security *survey* (184 papers, 2000–2020), **not** a DRL/adaptive-TSC attack survey as described in the brief. The substantive DRL-TSC-attack primary source is arXiv:2111.02845 (= ACM TIST 10.1145/3625236). Recommend citing 2111.02845/3625236 for the DRL-TSC attack claim and citing 2007.08041 only as a general CAV-attack taxonomy, if at all.
- **EMVLight ID note:** arXiv:2206.13441 is the multi-agent RL journal version (Transportation Research Part C, 2022); an earlier related preprint (arXiv:2109.05429, "EMVLight: A Decentralized Reinforcement Learning Framework for Efficient Passage of Emergency Vehicles") also exists. The brief's ID is correct for the primary MARL paper.
- **Trust assumptions are inferred where papers are silent.** For CoLLMLight and REG-TSC the *absence* of any trust/verification mechanism was verified by full-text reading (no "trust/spoof/malicious/compromised" language regarding inputs). For CoLight, LLMLight, SafeLight, iLLM-TSC, LA-Light, VLMLight and Max-Pressure, the trust assumption is *implicit* (inferred from the input pipeline and the absence of a threat model), not an explicit quoted statement — flagged as inference, not verbatim.
- **AMLAS author list and GSN/SACM version details** are cited partly from secondary summaries and the SCSC standard pages; the dissertation should confirm the exact AMLAS document version and GSN Standard v3 revision against the primary York/SCSC publications.
- **Reported performance/attack figures** ("up to 68.1%", "65% / 22.0 s→7.48 s", "17.5%", "20.4%", "42.6%") are the respective authors' own simulation results, not independently verified, and (for the attack figures) are specific to the tested configurations and traffic scenarios (notably the I-SIG configuration for the 68.1% figure).
