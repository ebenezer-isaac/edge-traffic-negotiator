\finding{The comparator decides the outcome.} Against the fixed-time floor
every arm significantly beats it on Old Street and none does on the other
two maps; against MaxPressure the only arm in this table to reach
significance is stock on the Bloomsbury grid ($+2.6\%$,
$p{=}1.3{\times}10^{-3}$), a harm and not a gain, though one catalogue
stock arm does beat MaxPressure on Old Street (\S\ref{sec:res-factorial}),
and the calibrated regime changes both pictures (\S\ref{sec:res-calib}).
Comparator-invariant is the negative that matters: a $+42$-point offline
accuracy gain produces no consistent delay change in these three regimes,
and the pooled effect is indistinguishable from zero under either
comparator, the \finding{delay-decoupling paradox} the decision battery
anticipated. Fine-tuning's one significant effect is protective: stock
control significantly \emph{harms} the congested Bloomsbury grid where the
fine-tuned controller is neutral, the head-to-head favouring fine-tuning by
$-1.8\%$ ($p{=}.022$). \S\ref{sec:dis-decoupling} takes up the mechanism:
masking confines both arms to one conflict-free menu and the
anti-starvation floor serves 23--61\% of ticks, though the floor's share
varies with the arm (\S\ref{sec:res-authorship}), so much served behaviour
is identical by construction whatever the proposal quality. I ran no
unmasked arm, so this is a design-level reading, not a measurement, and
the headroom may be small to begin with: external calibration puts the
whole LLM-vs-MaxPressure gap at 1--2\% \cite{dglight2026}.

Because paired $t$-tests on means hide the skew the catastrophic tail of
\S\ref{sec:res-forensics} puts into the per-seed distributions,
Table~\ref{tab:robust} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) adds 95\% CIs and a
Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the \emph{typical} seed. \finding{The Old
Street win holds on the typical seed} as well as the mean ($p\le.002$ both
tests); the Euston intervals are wide and zero-straddling on the topology
with the heaviest failure tail, so the characterisation is
\emph{typical-case behaviour with a catastrophic tail}, pointing at tail
control (a per-decision timeout, or an outlier detector reverting to the
classical policy) rather than at better average decisions. The rank-test
results are not confirmatory and would not survive a Holm correction
across the roughly forty paired comparisons in this report; the
fine-tuned-versus-stock head-to-head, the study's primary contrast by
design, does (Appendix~\ref{app:holm}).

The Phi-4-mini confirmation arm (final rows of
Table~\ref{tab:closedloop-full}) extends saturation end-to-end: the 3.8B
fine-tuned student's closed-loop profile is not distinguishable from the
0.6B's on either comparator, with the same divergence band (0.11 to 0.17)
and authorship profile, and on the calibrated grid the intervals bound any
difference within $\pm0.38\%$ of delay; it serves at the lowest latency of
my compiled arms (Table~\ref{tab:latency}, Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). On
the scale-threshold question that is a bounded non-difference, not a
demonstrated equivalence (\S\ref{sec:res-calib}).
