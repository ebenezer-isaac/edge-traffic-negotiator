\subsection{The fair re-test of the SLM authorisation negative}\label{sec:res-lee}
An early measured negative, the raw SLM losing to a deterministic template
on its legal-note job (citation-F1 0.12 retrieval-free and 0.34 with
retrieval against the template's 0.48 over 97 held-out notes), carried a
supervisor-posed objection (Stott): the model was guessing because the
policy was never in front of it. The re-test partially vindicates it
(balanced 26-claim authorisation dataset, four models $\times$
policy-scaffolded and bare arms, scored against the deterministic
knowledge-base gate; Appendix~\ref{app:lee}). Stock Phi-4-mini rises
from 42.3\% to 69.2\% class accuracy and its false-negative rate falls to
zero. Even that best cell makes \risk{four dangerous false grants}
(four of 18 claims that must be denied) where the gate makes none in
26/26, so policy grounding rescues recall, not safety. The \emph{fine-tuned}
students are far worse than their own stock bases, ft-Phi falling to
19.2\% scaffolded with 15 of 26 responses unparseable and ft-Qwen (the
generalist of \S\ref{sec:res-offline}) unable to emit the required schema
at all bare (26/26 parse failures); format capture by its training schema
is the natural reading, an inference and not a measurement. The retention
probe's mild MMLU decline (\S\ref{sec:dis-threats}) understates the cost
on near-neighbour structured tasks.
