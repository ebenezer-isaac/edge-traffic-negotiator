\subsection{Backpressure guarantees and their assumptions}\label{app:backpressure}
The four assumptions behind MaxPressure's throughput-optimality
\cite{varaiya2013maxpressure} that \S\ref{sec:bg-classical} says fail in
central London. First, the proof assumes unbounded queue storage: Gregoire
et al.\ \cite{gregoire2014backpressure} prove that linear-pressure control
of Varaiya's form is not work-conserving once queue capacities are finite,
construct explicit deadlocks, and repair it with a capacity-normalised
pressure that is \emph{not} part of standard MaxPressure; central London's
short links imply small capacities, so that failure condition is plausibly
standing here, though I do not measure it. Second, the only stability
result for a deployable (non-oracle) backpressure controller holds under
heavy load alone: Gregoire et al.\ \cite{gregoire2014routing} require
every queue to exceed saturation flow, concede that sub-saturation queues
``can unstabilize the queuing network'', and call stability outside it
``still a challenging problem''; measured diurnal demand sits below that
threshold most of the day, and the candidate mechanism there, the pressure
signal chasing arrival noise where a fixed cycle holds steady, I do not
test. Third, every favourable empirical figure is a grid figure (roughly
90\% of oracle backpressure on a uniform $21\times21$ grid, about 80\%
under heterogeneous parameters), and the authors caution that grid results
cannot extend to arbitrary networks. Fourth, junctions with give-way
movements, gap acceptance or gyratory circulation fall outside the model
domain: service in these theorems is binary and phase-determined, so no
guarantee applies.

\subsection{Seed families outside the disjoint set}\label{app:seedfamilies}
Three families of \S\ref{sec:met-disjoint} do not use the training-disjoint

\S\ref{ch:results} is a disjoint-seed number.

\subsection{Longest-queue-first control}\label{app:lqf}
A third classical baseline tests whether the results of
\S\ref{sec:res-maxpressure} are specific to \emph{pressure}.
Longest-queue-first serves the largest upstream queue with no downstream
term, so unlike MaxPressure it will discharge into a blocked exit, though
the two are routinely conflated. Over the same thirty seeds with shield,
minimum green, clearance and anti-starvation floor identical,
longest-queue-first is statistically indistinguishable from MaxPressure on
all five topologies ($-0.9\%$ to $+13.4\%$, every $p\ge.30$) and
reproduces its pattern against the naive floor: worse on the corridor
($+13.6\%$, $p{=}.027$), better on Old Street ($-5.9\%$) and the
calibrated grid ($-8.3\%$). The pressure formulation is not detectably
part of the classical weakness on a saturated arterial, though the
intervals are wide (Euston calibrated $[-12.7, +39.5]$), so this is a
non-rejection rather than a demonstrated equivalence; saturation, link
storage and the assumed turning split all remain candidates.

\subsection{Serving-artifact provenance}\label{app:provenance}
The nine-point gap of \S\ref{sec:res-factorial} is comparator-robust
though the sign flip is not: against the fixed-time floor the catalogue
and pipeline-compiled Qwen3-0.6B artifacts are $+17.4\%$ and $+4.8\%$,
both worse than the floor but 12.6 points apart, and the gap holds on two
of the three topologies where I could compare them (Old Street $-0.2\%$,
$p{=}.93$).

\subsection{Multiple comparisons}\label{app:holm}
The rank-test results of \S\ref{sec:res-closedloop} are not confirmatory:
of roughly forty paired comparisons across the report, the two reaching
$p<.05$ only under the rank test would not survive a Holm correction
across that family, and I pre-registered no confirmatory comparison. The
fine-tuned-versus-stock head-to-head is nonetheless the study's primary
contrast by design, the only cell in which a single variable is
manipulated, and at $p{=}2.0{\times}10^{-21}$ it survives Holm where they
do not.

\subsection{Calibration decision audit}\label{app:calib-audit}
Is the demand calibration of \S\ref{sec:res-calib} a principled correction
or a search for a more favourable test bed? In favour: the guard metrics
were recorded in every cell from the campaign's first run, so their values
were not produced by looking; the diagnosis is outcome-independent; and
the 40\% target matched Old Street's completion rate, not any effect size.
Against: the decision to look followed a weak result, a data-dependent
choice that retaining the superseded cells does not by itself redeem.
Uniform application is the check I can offer: Euston peak-hour also fails
the guard and is where SLM control looks worst (\S\ref{sec:res-factorial}),
so calibrating the corridor risked the conclusion rather than protecting
it, and I calibrated it, though the probed fractions bracketed failure only
on the grid. The rule, take the largest demand fraction that still clears,
is stated in the text rather than encoded in the calibration script, which
prints completion at each probed fraction and leaves the choice to the
analyst.

\subsection{Trajectory-level convergence detail}\label{app:convergence}
The identical cells of \S\ref{sec:res-forensics} are separate runs serving
the same phase sequence, not duplicated records: wall-clock differs by up
to 2.15$\times$ and median latency by 2.23$\times$, and in one both arms
took 307 decisions with SLM/floor attribution differing by exactly one
tick, where floor and model chose the same phase. Of the 14 catastrophic
cells, five are the stock arm alone, one the 3.8B student alone, and eight
are four seed pairs on which both fine-tuned students fail together with
per-seed relative delay agreeing to within 0.1--0.2 points; that pairing
restates the convergence rather than explaining it, and the independent
evidence is the decision-level agreement.

\subsection{Completeness layer}\label{app:ct}
Above the per-run chain sits the Certificate-Transparency-style
completeness layer of \S\ref{sec:bg-audit}. Over a twelve-entry batch, a
salted Merkle root hides the entry contents while remaining provable, the
salt is erasable off-chain, and an inclusion proof verifies while the same
proof under a \emph{forged} salt is rejected; under a two-witness
cross-audit the witnesses agree, and when one is made to serve a different
root the equivocation is detected and consistency fails, a witness
refusing to overwrite its own history. The cross-auditor's own honesty is
an unverified assumption, on a par with the identity root, and the batch is
not externally anchored: I withheld the public-log write rather than
pollute a production transparency log.

\subsection{Trust-coefficient battery detail}\label{app:trust}
The property battery of \S\ref{sec:res-trust} (9/9): a persistently honest
junction climbs to 0.929 trust, a persistent liar collapses to 0.011 and
loses the power to corroborate, the designed asymmetry holds in both
directions, and a truthful-but-late report is vindicated retroactively by
local sensing. In closed-loop traffic (honest, blatant and stealth
misreporting injected into SUMO) the battery ran against fine-tuned 3.8B,
stock 0.6B and fine-tuned 0.6B controllers. Trust gating was off, so the
ledger altered no served phase: detection is scored by replaying each
run's recorded claims and local-sensing confirmations through the ledger,
measuring what it would have caught rather than what it prevented, and the
delay costs are the attack's effect on an undefended controller. No
stock-3.8B run exists, so the stock/fine-tuned contrast is available at
0.6B only. The battery runs three seeds ($42, 123, 31$) in the
coordination format against the same-seed MaxPressure baseline; 42 and 123
are training-state seeds and 31 a disjoint evaluation seed
(\S\ref{sec:met-disjoint}), making this the one family whose fine-tuned
arms partly see states the students were distilled from. Every controller
catches and collapses a blatant liar on Bloomsbury (62--70 catches, final
trust 0.21--0.27) and grid4$\times$4 (146--151 catches, trust $\le$0.03,
corroboration stripped), while a calibrated stealth attacker evades
essentially everywhere ($\le$2 catches, trust $\ge$0.92); on the
daily-demand corridor, Euston peak-hour and Old Street the liar junction is
never consulted, so the mechanism is inert there. Blatant lying adds $+2.7$ to $+11.5$ points
over the same controller's honest run on the synthetic grids even as the
liar's trust collapses; on Bloomsbury the damage stays within $\sim$3
points (fine-tuned qwen $-1.3\%$ under attack against $-1.1\%$ honest),
which the design predicts is the local-sensing override, but no
override-ablated arm was run, so map structure is an equally live
explanation. Stealth's intermittent inflations cost $-2.3$ to $+2.9$
points, buying retained corroboration power at high trust to abuse later.

\subsection{Emergency-vehicle priority detail}\label{app:ev-detail}
The three modes of \S\ref{sec:res-ev} are \emph{nopreempt} (no signal
priority, as at an installation without emergency-vehicle detection),
\emph{naive} (preempts on any claim), and \emph{defended} (preempts only on
a claim with a valid authorising key and corroboration,
\S\ref{sec:des-trust}). Table~\ref{tab:ev} gives the four quantities with
95\% CIs over thirty seeds. Whether the exchange of 8.0\,s of ambulance
time for the protection the gate buys is worth making is a policy
judgement.

\subsection{Authorisation re-test detail}\label{app:lee}
The legal-note pilot of \S\ref{sec:res-lee} ran at 37.8\,s per note, and
its gold is policy-base-derived rather than human-graded, so that negative
is a pilot proxy. The objection held that the negative could not stand
until the SLM was re-tested with explicit in-prompt rules and a
reason-then-classify structure, which the scaffolded arm supplies. The
required output schema was \texttt{\{"class", "grant"\}}; the fine-tuned
students' training schema was \texttt{\{"phase": N\}}. The harness did not
retain raw responses, so what ft-Qwen emitted instead of the schema cannot
be shown.

\subsection{Residual legal exposure}\label{app:legal}
The audit scenarios use synthetic actors on real topology, so no real data
subject exists, but a real deployment would face two residual exposures
(\S\ref{sec:dis-threats}): curating which rules apply is itself a
judgement about relevance, so the evidence pack cannot be treated as a
neutral readout, and I cite no authority for that proposition because I
know of none; and the pack attracts only qualified privilege, which
disclosure defeats.

\subsection{Serving reliability and latency detail}\label{app:serving}
The three reliability findings of \S\ref{sec:res-reliability} in full: one
long offline evaluation died at 5{,}500 of 5{,}580 rows on a host
\texttt{OSError(28)} (no space left on device) and had to be re-run;
registering builder-compiled models required undocumented synthesis of the
inference manifest; and the catalogue Qwen3-0.6B GPU artifact produced
malformed output after extended service uptime, an unlogged operational
observation rather than a measured finding, and the reason
\S\ref{sec:met-baselines} takes the pipeline-compiled artifact as the
control. Table~\ref{tab:latency} summarises decision latency. The 3.8B
student is the fastest of my pipeline-compiled arms and the 0.6B students
the slowest, a counter-intuitive ordering that is a property of the
compiled artifacts rather than of parameter count and holds only within my
own compiles: four vendor catalogue arms are faster still, including the
catalogue build of the student's own weights at 0.68\,s. One call in the
0.6B arms exceeded the 10\,s decision interval; phi-4-mini-reasoning
differs only in frequency, a lone outlier here against a p99 above the
interval there.
